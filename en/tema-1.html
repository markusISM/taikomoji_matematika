Key Concepts

1

What is the slope of a line?

The slope \(m\) measures the steepness of the line. The slope is a measure of the rate of change of \(y\) with respect to \(x\).

2

How to compute the slope from two points?

Take two points of the line, \(A(x_1;\,y_1)\) and \(B(x_2;\,y_2)\):

\[ m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \tan\alpha \]
Example Points \(A(3;\,46000)\) and \(B(7;\,26000)\): \[ \begin{aligned} m &= \frac{26000 - 46000}{7 - 3} \\ &= \frac{-20000}{4} = -5000 \end{aligned} \]
?Why?

\(\Delta y = y_2 - y_1\) is how much \(y\) has changed, and \(\Delta x = x_2 - x_1\) is how much \(x\) has changed, when going from point \(A\) to point \(B\). Dividing \(\Delta y\) by \(\Delta x\) gives how much \(y\) changes when \(x\) increases by 1 unit. This is the slope.

Both differences are computed in the same order: the coordinate of point \(A\) is subtracted from the coordinate of point \(B\).

\(\alpha\) is the angle marked in the figure, so the slope can also be written as \(\tan\alpha\).

3

What is the intercept?

The intercept \(b\) determines the line's position on the vertical axis. It equals the value of \(y\) at \(x = 0\).

The line crosses the vertical axis at the point \((0;\,b)\).

4

How to compute the intercept?

Once the slope \(m\) is known, take any point of the line, \(A(x_1;\,y_1)\):

\[ b = -m x_1 + y_1 \]
Example Continuing: \(m = -5000\), \(A(3;\,46000)\). \[ \begin{aligned} b &= -(-5000)\cdot 3 + 46000 \\ &= 15000 + 46000 = 61000 \end{aligned} \]

If a point of the line with \(x = 0\) is known, no calculation is needed: \(b\) equals the \(y\) value of that point.

?Why?

Point \(A\) lies on the line, so its coordinates satisfy the equation \(y = mx + b\):

\[ y_1 = m x_1 + b \]

Moving \(m x_1\) to the other side of the equation gives \(b = -m x_1 + y_1\).

5

How to write the slope-intercept form?

\[ y = mx + b \]
  1. Compute the slope \(m\) from two points.
  2. Compute the intercept \(b\).
  3. Substitute \(m\) and \(b\) into the equation \(y = mx + b\).
Example \(m = -5000\), \(b = 61000\): \[ y = -5000x + 61000 \]
6

What does the equation show?

  • \(m\)the steepness of the line: how much \(y\) changes when \(x\) increases by 1 unit;
  • \(b\)the line's position on the vertical axis: the value of \(y\) at \(x = 0\).
Application example In a linear depreciation problem, the value equation of a machine is \(V(t) = -5000t + 61000\): its value falls by 5000 EUR every year \((m = -5000)\), and its original value is 61000 EUR \((b = 61000)\).
