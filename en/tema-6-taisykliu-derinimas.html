Combining the Rules

In this section you will find examples of calculating derivatives in which several rules have to be combined in one function, together with practice problems. Try to solve each problem yourself first, then reveal the solution one step at a time.

  • Examples

The rules used

The product rule

\[ \begin{aligned} \big(u(x)\cdot v(x)\big)' &= u'(x)\cdot v(x) \\ &\phantom{=}\; + u(x)\cdot v'(x) \end{aligned} \]

The quotient rule

\[ \left(\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}\right)' = \frac{u'(x)v(x) - u(x)v'(x)}{v^2(x)} \]

The chain rule

\[ \big(g(u(x))\big)' = g'\big(u(x)\big)\cdot u'(x) \]

The rules are explained in the product rule, quotient rule and chain rule cards on the Topic 6 page.

Examples

These examples combine the chain rule with the product or quotient rule. First identify the structure of the whole function and write the corresponding rule. Then use the chain rule wherever a factor, numerator or denominator is itself a composite function.

Example 1

A product of a power and an exponential function

Problem Find \(f'(x)\) if \(f(x) = x^2e^{-3x}\).

1The rules

The factors are \(x^2\) and \(e^{-3x}\), so start with the product rule. Its first term differentiates the power and keeps the exponential unchanged. Its second term keeps the power unchanged and differentiates the exponential. For that derivative, use the chain rule: the inner function is \(-3x\), with derivative \(-3\). Multiply the unchanged exponential by this inner derivative.

2Differentiation

\[ f'(x) = 2x\cdot e^{-3x} + x^2\cdot e^{-3x}\cdot(-3) \]

3Simplifying

Finally, factor out the common exponential and one power of the input, \(xe^{-3x}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 2xe^{-3x} - 3x^2e^{-3x} \\ &= xe^{-3x}(2 - 3x) \end{aligned} \]
Answer \[ f'(x) = xe^{-3x}(2 - 3x) \]
Example 2

A product of the input and a root

Problem Find \(f'(x)\) if \(f(x) = x\sqrt{x^2 + 1}\).

1The rules

Apply the product rule to \(x\) and \(\sqrt{x^2 + 1}\). The derivative of the first factor is one, giving the unchanged square root as the first term. For the second term, keep the first factor and differentiate the square root using the chain rule. Its inner function is \(x^2 + 1\), with derivative \(2x\). Divide the inner derivative by twice the original square root, then simplify the numerical factors.

2Differentiation

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 1\cdot\sqrt{x^2 + 1} + x\cdot\frac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \\ &= \sqrt{x^2 + 1} + \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \end{aligned} \]

3Simplifying

To combine the terms, use the common denominator \(\sqrt{x^2 + 1}\). Multiply the first term by this denominator divided by itself. The two square roots in its numerator multiply to give their radicand. Then add the numerators:

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= \frac{x^2 + 1 + x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \\ &= \frac{2x^2 + 1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \end{aligned} \]
Answer \[ f'(x) = \frac{2x^2 + 1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \]
Example 3

A quotient of an exponential function and a binomial

Problem Find \(f'(x)\) if \(f(x) = \dfrac{e^{2x}}{x + 1}\).

1The rules

The denominator depends on \(x\), so apply the quotient rule. Differentiate the numerator using the chain rule: \((e^{2x})' = 2e^{2x}\). The factor two comes from differentiating the exponent. The denominator has derivative one. Substitute both derivatives into the quotient rule, retaining the subtraction sign and squaring the original denominator. The function and its derivative are undefined at \(x = -1\).

2Differentiation

\[ f'(x) = \frac{2e^{2x}(x + 1) - e^{2x}\cdot 1}{(x + 1)^2} \]

3Simplifying

Factor \(e^{2x}\) out of both terms in the numerator. Inside the brackets, expand the product and combine the constant terms:

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= \frac{e^{2x}(2x + 2 - 1)}{(x + 1)^2} \\ &= \frac{e^{2x}(2x + 1)}{(x + 1)^2} \end{aligned} \]

The exponential remains in the answer because it is not a common factor of the numerator and denominator.

Answer \[ f'(x) = \frac{e^{2x}(2x + 1)}{(x + 1)^2} \]

Practice problems

Write the solutions out as in the examples: identify the structure of the function, choose the rule, differentiate and simplify the result. Then check your answer.

Find \(f'(x)\) if \(f(x) = e^{2x}\ln x\).

\(f'(x) = e^{2x}\left(2\ln x + \dfrac{1}{x}\right)\)

Find \(f'(x)\) if \(f(x) = x\sqrt{3x + 1}\).

\(f'(x) = \dfrac{9x + 2}{2\sqrt{3x + 1}}\)
