What is market equilibrium

Demand (\(D\)) describes the buyers: consumers buy more only if the price falls, so the demand line falls.

Supply (\(S\)) describes the producers: producers offer more only if the price rises, so the supply line rises.

Market equilibrium is the point \(E\) where these lines intersect. At the equilibrium price \(p_e\), the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, \(x_e\). This is the quantity at which buyers and sellers agree.

The lines are drawn for the electric scooter example solved below.

The model

The quantity is always \(x\) and the unit price is always \(p\), whatever order the sentence names them in. Both relations are written in the slope-intercept form:

Demand

\[ p = ax + b, \quad a < 0 \]

Supply

\[ p = cx + d, \quad c > 0 \]

Equilibrium

\[ D = S: \quad ax + b = cx + d \]
?What do \(b\) and \(d\) mean?

\(b\) is the price at which nothing is demanded, and \(d\) is the price at which supply begins.

?Why do the signs of the slopes matter?

The signs are part of the model: consumers buy more only if the price falls (\(a < 0\)), producers offer more only if it rises (\(c > 0\)). The sign of the slope is therefore a check on the work: a negative slope for a supply equation means that a mistake has been made somewhere.

How to find the equilibrium

  1. Write the buyers’ (demand) data and the sellers’ (supply) data separately, each as two points \((x;\,p)\), or as one point and the changes \(\Delta p\), \(\Delta x\).
  2. Find the demand function \(p = ax + b\).
  3. Find the supply function \(p = cx + d\).
  4. Check the signs: \(a < 0\) and \(c > 0\).
  5. Equate the two right-hand sides and solve for \(x_e\).
  6. Substitute \(x_e\) into both functions: they must return the same \(p_e\).
?How to read the two points out of the problem?

A straight line is determined by two points, and a problem always contains two. They are given in one of the following ways (the examples are from the electric scooter problem):

  • Two pairs stated directly. “The quantity demanded is 2000 units when the unit price is 480 euros. At a unit price of 360 euros, the quantity demanded increases to 5000 units.” The points are \((2000;\,480)\) and \((5000;\,360)\) – the quantity first, the price second.
  • One pair and a rate of change. “For each 60 euros increase in the unit price above 160 euros, the manufacturer will market an additional 1000 units.” Applying the change once to the point \((0;\,160)\) gives the second point \((1000;\,220)\).
    • The second point is not necessary. Knowing the changes, we get the slope \(\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta x}\) at once, and the coordinates of the given point are used to find the intercept. For the scooter supply \(\Delta p = 60\), \(\Delta x = 1000\), so \(c = \dfrac{60}{1000} = 0.06\), and the intercept is found from the point \((0;\,160)\).
  • A price at which nothing is offered. “The manufacturer will not market any scooters if the price is 160 euros or lower.” This gives the point \((0;\,160)\): at that price the quantity supplied is zero, so this point lies on the price axis and its price is at once the intercept \(d\) of the supply line.
!Common mistakes
  • The order of the coordinates. The quantity is the first coordinate and the price the second, whatever order the sentence names them in.
  • Mixed data. When one sentence gives both the buyers’ and the sellers’ data, sort the numbers into two pairs of points before calculating.
  • Signs. A negative slope for a supply equation means that a mistake has been made somewhere.
  • Check. Substituting \(x_e\) into both functions must give the same price \(p_e\).
What you must write in the exam
  1. When introducing new variables not specified in lectures or the problem, or when changing measurement units, this must be explicitly written in the solution.
  2. If the problem includes several questions, answers to all of them must be clearly presented.
  3. When solving problems involving finding demand/supply or other linear functions, it is necessary to indicate the points or other information used to determine the parameters.

Step by step

We solve one problem in three parts: the demand function, the supply function and the equilibrium.

Problem The quantity demanded of a certain model of electric scooter is 2000 units when the unit price is 480 euros. At a unit price of 360 euros, the quantity demanded increases to 5000 units. The manufacturer will not market any scooters if the price is 160 euros or lower. However, for each 60 euros increase in the unit price above 160 euros, the manufacturer will market an additional 1000 units. Both the demand and the supply equations are known to be linear. Find the equilibrium quantity and price.
Part 1

Finding the demand function

The buyers’ data The quantity demanded is 2000 units when the unit price is 480 euros. At a unit price of 360 euros, the quantity demanded increases to 5000 units.

1Two points

The quantity is the first coordinate and the price is the second: \((x_1;\,p_1) = (2000;\,480)\), \((x_2;\,p_2) = (5000;\,360)\).

2

\[ \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{p_2 - p_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{360 - 480}{5000 - 2000} \\ &= \frac{-120}{3000} = -0.04 \end{aligned} \]

The slope is negative, as it must be for demand.

3The intercept

\[ \begin{aligned} b &= -a x_1 + p_1 = 0.04\cdot 2000 + 480 \\ &= 80 + 480 = 560 \end{aligned} \]
The demand function \[ p = -0.04x + 560 \]
?The logic of the demand function: quantity ↔ price

The demand function \(p = -0.04x + 560\) links the quantity and the price, so knowing one of them we can find the other.

A quantity is given, the price is asked for. Put the quantity in place of \(x\) and compute \(p\) directly; no equation has to be solved. For \(x = 5000\):

\[ \begin{aligned} p &= -0.04\cdot 5000 + 560 \\ &= -200 + 560 = 360 \end{aligned} \]

When the quantity demanded is 5000 units, the price is 360 euros – as in the problem.

A price is given, the quantity is asked for. Put the price in place of \(p\) and solve the equation for \(x\). For \(p = 480\):

\[ \begin{aligned} 480 &= -0.04x + 560 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 0.04x &= 80 \\ \Longrightarrow\; x &= 2000 \end{aligned} \]

When the price is 480 euros, the quantity demanded is 2000 units – as in the problem.

The intercept \(b = 560\) is the price at which nothing is demanded.

Part 2

Finding the supply function

The manufacturer’s data The manufacturer will not market any scooters if the price is 160 euros or lower. However, for each 60 euros increase in the unit price above 160 euros, the manufacturer will market an additional 1000 units.

1A point and the changes

Nothing is offered at the price of 160 euros, so the line passes through the point \((x_1;\,p_1) = (0;\,160)\). An increase of 60 euros in the price brings 1000 more units to the market: \(\Delta p = 60\), \(\Delta x = 1000\).

2The slope from the changes

\[ c = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta x} = \frac{60}{1000} = 0.06 \]

The slope is positive, as it must be for supply.

⇄Another way: through the second point

Applying the change once gives the second point \((0 + 1000;\,160 + 60) = (1000;\,220)\), and the slope is computed by the two-point formula:

\[ \begin{aligned} c &= \frac{p_2 - p_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{220 - 160}{1000 - 0} \\ &= \frac{60}{1000} = 0.06 \end{aligned} \]

The slope is the same.

3The intercept

The point \((0;\,160)\) lies on the price axis, so its price is the intercept itself:

\[ \begin{aligned} d &= -c x_1 + p_1 = -0.06\cdot 0 + 160 \\ &= 160 \end{aligned} \]
The supply function \[ p = 0.06x + 160 \]
?The logic of the supply function: quantity ↔ price

The supply function \(p = 0.06x + 160\) also lets us find one of the two values from the other.

A quantity is given, the price is asked for. For \(x = 1000\):

\[ \begin{aligned} p &= 0.06\cdot 1000 + 160 \\ &= 60 + 160 = 220 \end{aligned} \]

When the quantity supplied is 1000 units, the price is 220 euros – as in the problem.

A price is given, the quantity is asked for. For \(p = 160\):

\[ \begin{aligned} 160 &= 0.06x + 160 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 0.06x &= 0 \\ \Longrightarrow\; x &= 0 \end{aligned} \]

At the price of 160 euros no scooters are offered – as in the problem.

The intercept \(d = 160\) is the price at which supply begins.

Part 3

Finding the equilibrium

We have Demand \(p = -0.04x + 560\), supply \(p = 0.06x + 160\).

1Demand equals supply

\[ -0.04x + 560 = 0.06x + 160 \]

2The equilibrium quantity

\[ 400 = 0.1x \;\Longrightarrow\; x_e = 4000 \]

3The equilibrium price

\[ \begin{aligned} p_e &= 0.06\cdot 4000 + 160 \\ &= 240 + 160 = 400 \end{aligned} \]

4Check

Substitute \(x_e\) into the demand function – it must give the same price:

\[ \begin{aligned} &{-}0.04\cdot 4000 + 560 \\ &= -160 + 560 = 400 \end{aligned} \]
Answer The equilibrium quantity is 4000 scooters and the equilibrium price is 400 euros.

Examples: the demand or the supply function

A problem often asks for only one of the two functions and then adds a second question: what price corresponds to a given quantity, or what quantity corresponds to a given price. Try to solve the problem yourself first.

Example 1 · Demand

Wireless earbuds

Problem Assume that the demand equation for a certain model of wireless earbuds has the form \(p = ax + b\), where \(x\) is the quantity demanded and \(p\) is the unit price in euros. Suppose the quantity demanded is 4000 units when the unit price is 60 euros and 9000 units when the unit price is 35 euros. Determine the demand function. What is the quantity demanded when the unit price is 50 euros?

1Two points

The quantity is the first coordinate and the price the second, so \((x_1;\,p_1) = (4000;\,60)\), \((x_2;\,p_2) = (9000;\,35)\).

2The slope

\[ \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{p_2 - p_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{35 - 60}{9000 - 4000} \\ &= \frac{-25}{5000} = -0.005 \end{aligned} \]

The slope is negative, as it must be for demand: one extra unit can be sold only if the price falls by half a cent.

3The intercept

\[ \begin{aligned} b &= -a x_1 + p_1 = 0.005\cdot 4000 + 60 \\ &= 20 + 60 = 80 \end{aligned} \]

4The demand function

\[ p = -0.005x + 80 \]

5The quantity demanded at the price of 50 euros

Here the price is given, so \(p = 50\) is substituted and the equation is solved for \(x\):

\[ \begin{aligned} 50 &= -0.005x + 80 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 0.005x &= 30 \\ \Longrightarrow\; x &= \frac{30}{0.005} = 6000 \end{aligned} \]
Answer The demand function is \(p = -0.005x + 80\); at the unit price of 50 euros 6000 pairs of earbuds are demanded.
Example 2 · Supply

Office chairs

Problem Suppliers of a certain model of office chair will not make any chairs available in the market if the unit price is 90 euros or lower. However, for each 15 euros increase in the unit price above 90 euros, the suppliers will make an additional 600 chairs available. Assuming that the relationship between the unit price and the quantity supplied is linear, determine the supply function and compute the unit price at which the suppliers will make 3200 chairs available.

1A point and the changes

Nothing is offered at the price of 90 euros, which gives the point \((x_1;\,p_1) = (0;\,90)\). Raising the price by 15 euros brings 600 chairs to the market: \(\Delta p = 15\), \(\Delta x = 600\).

2The slope from the changes

When the changes are known, the second point is not necessary – the slope is the ratio of the change in price to the change in quantity:

\[ c = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta x} = \frac{15}{600} = 0.025 \]

The slope is positive, as it must be for supply.

⇄Another way: through the second point

Raising the price by 15 euros brings 600 chairs to the market, which gives the second point \((x_2;\,p_2) = (600;\,105)\). The slope by the two-point formula:

\[ \begin{aligned} c &= \frac{p_2 - p_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{105 - 90}{600 - 0} \\ &= \frac{15}{600} = 0.025 \end{aligned} \]

The slope is the same.

3The intercept

The first point lies on the price axis, so its price is the intercept itself. This can be seen from the formula by substituting the point \((0;\,90)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} d &= -c x_1 + p_1 = -0.025\cdot 0 + 90 \\ &= 90 \end{aligned} \]

Since \(x_1 = 0\), the term \(-c x_1\) is zero and only the price of the point is left. In other words, at \(x = 0\) the equation \(p = cx + d\) gives \(p = d\).

4The supply function

\[ p = 0.025x + 90 \]

5The price at which 3200 chairs are offered

Here the quantity is given, so \(x = 3200\) is substituted and the price is computed directly:

\[ p = 0.025\cdot 3200 + 90 = 80 + 90 = 170 \]
Answer The supply function is \(p = 0.025x + 90\); the suppliers will make 3200 chairs available at the unit price of 170 euros.

Examples: market equilibrium

Example 3

Fitness trackers

Problem The quantity demanded of a fitness tracker is 2400 units per week at the unit price of 90 euros, and for each decrease of the price by 5 euros the quantity demanded increases by 200 units. Suppliers will not market any trackers at 30 euros or lower, but at 78 euros they make 3200 units available. Both relations are linear. Determine the demand and supply functions and find the equilibrium price and quantity.

1Demand

One point, \((2400;\,90)\), and a rate of change are given. The price falls while the quantity rises, so the slope is negative:

\[ a = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta x} = \frac{-5}{200} = -0.025 \]

The intercept, using \((2400;\,90)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} b &= -(-0.025)\cdot 2400 + 90 \\ &= 60 + 90 = 150 \\ &\Longrightarrow\; p = -0.025x + 150 \end{aligned} \]

2Supply

The boundary condition gives the point \((0;\,30)\), the second statement the point \((3200;\,78)\). The slope:

\[ c = \frac{78 - 30}{3200 - 0} = \frac{48}{3200} = 0.015 \]

The intercept, using \((0;\,30)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} d &= -0.015\cdot 0 + 30 = 30 \\ &\Longrightarrow\; p = 0.015x + 30 \end{aligned} \]

3Equilibrium

\[ \begin{aligned} &{-}0.025x + 150 = 0.015x + 30 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& 120 = 0.04x \\ \Longrightarrow\;& x_e = 3000 \end{aligned} \]

4Check

\(0.015\cdot 3000 + 30 = 75\) and \(-0.025\cdot 3000 + 150 = 75\) euros.

Answer Demand \(p = -0.025x + 150\), supply \(p = 0.015x + 30\); the market clears at 3000 trackers per week and the price of 75 euros.
Example 4

Market equilibrium

Problem At a price of 12 euros the weekly supply of a product is 900 units and the quantity demanded is 1500 units. At a price of 18 euros the supply is 1500 units and the quantity demanded is 1200 units. Both relations are linear. Find the supply and demand functions and the equilibrium price and quantity.

1Sorting the data

Sort the four numbers into two pairs of points before calculating. Supply: \((900;\,12)\) and \((1500;\,18)\). Demand: \((1500;\,12)\) and \((1200;\,18)\).

2Supply

\[ c = \frac{18 - 12}{1500 - 900} = \frac{6}{600} = 0.01 \] \[ \begin{aligned} d &= -0.01\cdot 900 + 12 \\ &= -9 + 12 = 3 \\ &\Longrightarrow\; p = 0.01x + 3 \end{aligned} \]

3Demand

\[ a = \frac{18 - 12}{1200 - 1500} = \frac{6}{-300} = -0.02 \] \[ \begin{aligned} b &= -(-0.02)\cdot 1500 + 12 \\ &= 30 + 12 = 42 \\ &\Longrightarrow\; p = -0.02x + 42 \end{aligned} \]

4Equilibrium

\[ \begin{aligned} &0.01x + 3 = -0.02x + 42 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& 0.03x = 39 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& x_e = 1300, \quad p_e = 16 \end{aligned} \]
Answer Supply \(p = 0.01x + 3\), demand \(p = -0.02x + 42\); equilibrium at 1300 units and 16 euros.

Check yourself

At market equilibrium, which condition holds?

  1. Quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.
  2. Quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
  3. Quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, at the equilibrium price.
  4. Price is always zero.

The demand for handmade soap bars is \(p = -0.03x + 50\) (\(p\) in €, \(x\) in bars) and the supply is \(p = 0.02x + 10\). What is the equilibrium quantity \(x\)?

  1. 800 bars
  2. 400 bars
  3. 1000 bars
  4. 600 bars
Equating the two prices, \(-0.03x + 50 = 0.02x + 10\), gives \(40 = 0.05x\), so \(x = 800\).

The original supply of a seasonal fruit is \(p = 0.04x + 2\). A poor harvest shifts the supply to \(p = 0.04x + 5\) (same slope, higher intercept), while demand stays \(p = -0.02x + 20\). How does the equilibrium quantity change, from before the shift to after?

  1. Increases from 300 to 250
  2. Decreases from 300 to 250
  3. Stays at 300
  4. Decreases from 250 to 200
Before the shift, \(0.04x + 2 = -0.02x + 20\) gives \(x = 300\); after the shift, \(0.04x + 5 = -0.02x + 20\) gives \(x = 250\).

Practice problems

Write the solutions out exactly as in the examples, then check your answer.

The quantity demanded of a certain brand of electric kettle is 1200 per week when the unit price is 48 euros. For each decrease in the unit price of 5 euros below 48 euros, the quantity demanded increases by 250 units. Assuming that the relationship between the unit price and the quantity demanded is linear, determine the demand function and compute the unit price at which 2000 kettles a week will be demanded.

\(p = -0.02x + 72\); 2000 kettles a week are demanded at the unit price of 32 euros.

Suppliers of a certain type of solar panel will make 5000 panels available in the market if the unit price is 120 euros. At a unit price of 150 euros, 11000 panels will be made available. Assuming that the relationship between the unit price and the quantity supplied is linear, determine the supply function and compute the quantity suppliers will make available when the unit price is 190 euros.

\(p = 0.005x + 95\); at the unit price of 190 euros the suppliers will make 19000 panels available.

For a new line of smartwatches, market research shows that 4000 units are demanded when the price is 45 €, and 2000 units are demanded when the price is 65 €. On the supply side, producers are willing to supply 500 units at 15 € and 2500 units at 35 €. Find the equilibrium quantity and price.

3750 units, 47.5 €.

Demand:

\[ \begin{aligned} a &= \frac{65 - 45}{2000 - 4000} = -0.01 \\ b &= 45 - (-0.01)\cdot 4000 = 85 \end{aligned} \]

so \(p = -0.01x + 85\).

Supply:

\[ \begin{aligned} c &= \frac{35 - 15}{2500 - 500} = 0.01 \\ d &= 15 - 0.01\cdot 500 = 10 \end{aligned} \]

so \(p = 0.01x + 10\).

\[ \begin{aligned} &{-}0.01x + 85 = 0.01x + 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& 75 = 0.02x \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 3750 \end{aligned} \]

\(p = 0.01\cdot 3750 + 10 = 47.5\) €.
