The Pace of Change

In this section the second derivative is applied to economic quantities: it shows whether a quantity changes faster and faster or more and more slowly, and by how much its rate of change will change when the input increases by one unit.

The second derivative as the pace of change

The first derivative is the rate at which a quantity changes. It is a function too, so it has a rate of change of its own: the second derivative. So \(f''(x)\) measures the pace of change: how quickly the rate of change itself rises or falls. You can see what this looks like in the interactive graph on the Topic 7 page.

The first derivative is interpreted in the usual way, and the second one in the same way, only it refers to the rate of change:

If \(x\) increases by one unit from \(x_0\), the quantity changes by approximately \(f'(x_0)\).
If \(x\) increases by one unit from \(x_0\), the rate of change changes by approximately \(f''(x_0)\).

Both values are approximate, and for the same reason: they are measured at the single point \(x_0\), and over a whole unit step the rates do not stay the same.

The sign

Only the sign of the second derivative is needed to interpret it:

  • if \(f''(x) > 0\), then \(f'(x)\) is increasing: the rate of change of the quantity increases;
  • if \(f''(x) < 0\), then \(f'(x)\) is decreasing: the rate of change of the quantity decreases.

What the sign does not mean. A negative second derivative does not mean that the quantity itself is falling. It means that the rate of change of the quantity is decreasing. Whether the quantity rises or falls is decided by the first derivative alone. So read the signs of both derivatives together: for example, if \(f'(x_0) < 0\) and \(f''(x_0) > 0\), the quantity is falling, but more and more slowly (see Example 3).

How to solve

  1. Write down the function and say what the variable means. If the quantity is not given by a formula, build it first.
  2. Differentiate. Before differentiating, write roots and reciprocals as powers. Name the rule that is being used and the functions it is applied to.
  3. Simplify the first derivative, and again write it as a power if that makes the next step easier.
  4. Differentiate the simplified first derivative and simplify the result.
  5. Substitute the value of the variable the problem names into \(f'\) and into \(f''\), and interpret both results.
The interpretation. The answer is not finished when the two numbers have been computed. Every problem of this kind asks for them to be explained in the words of the problem, and that explanation is part of the answer. Two sentences are needed: one saying how the quantity changes when the variable increases by one unit, and one saying how the rate of change itself changes.
!Common mistakes
  • A negative second derivative read as “the quantity is falling”. A negative second derivative means that the rate of change is decreasing. Whether the quantity rises or falls is shown by the first derivative.
  • Skipping the simplification. A first derivative that has not been simplified is harder to differentiate a second time.
  • Roots and fractions not written as powers. A derivative written as a root or a fraction is often harder to differentiate than the same expression written as a power.
  • Only numbers in the answer. Both results have to be interpreted: how the quantity changes and how its rate of change changes.

Examples

Try to solve the problem yourself first, then reveal the solution one step at a time.

Example 1

Marginal cost at a chemical plant

Problem A chemical plant’s daily total cost of producing \(x\) tonnes is
\[ TC(x) = 0.02x^3 - 1.2x^2 + 40x + 500 \]
euros. Find the marginal cost at an output of 10 tonnes a day and the rate at which that marginal cost is changing. Interpret both results.

1The first derivative

The function is a sum of powers, so the power rule is applied to each term (the sum and difference rule), and the derivative of the constant 500 is zero:

\[ \begin{aligned} MC(x) &= TC'(x) \\ &= 0.02\cdot 3x^2 - 1.2\cdot 2x + 40 \\ &= 0.06x^2 - 2.4x + 40 \end{aligned} \]

2The second derivative

The first derivative is again a sum of powers, so the same rule is applied again:

\[ TC''(x) = 0.06\cdot 2x - 2.4 = 0.12x - 2.4 \]

3The values

\[ \begin{aligned} MC(10) &= 0.06\cdot 100 - 2.4\cdot 10 + 40 \\ &= 6 - 24 + 40 = 22 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} TC''(10) &= 0.12\cdot 10 - 2.4 \\ &= 1.2 - 2.4 = -1.2 \end{aligned} \]
Answer The marginal cost is 22 euros a tonne, and it is changing by \(-1.2\) euros a tonne for each further tonne. If the output increases by one tonne from 10 tonnes a day, the total cost will grow by approximately 22 euros. If the output increases by one tonne from 10 tonnes a day, the marginal cost itself will fall by approximately 1.20 euros. So the plant still pays more for every extra tonne, but each extra tonne costs a little less than the one before it.
Example 2

Marginal product of labour in a workshop

Problem A workshop employing \(x\) workers produces \[ Q(x) = 60\sqrt{x} \] units an hour. Find the marginal product of labour at 25 workers and the rate at which it is changing. Interpret both results.

1The root as a power

First write the root as a power. By the rule \(\sqrt[n]{x^m} = x^{\frac{m}{n}}\) the square root of \(x\) is \(\sqrt{x} = \sqrt{x^1} = x^{\frac{1}{2}} = x^{0.5}\), so

\[ Q(x) = 60x^{0.5} \]

2The first derivative

Now the power rule applies: the exponent comes down in front and is then lowered by one.

\[ \begin{aligned} Q'(x) &= 60\cdot 0.5x^{0.5 - 1} = 30x^{-0.5} \\ &= \frac{30}{x^{0.5}} = \frac{30}{\sqrt{x}} \end{aligned} \]

3The second derivative

The first derivative is already written as a power, \(30x^{-0.5}\), so the power rule is applied to it in the same way:

\[ \begin{aligned} Q''(x) &= 30\cdot(-0.5)x^{-0.5 - 1} = -15x^{-1.5} \\ &= -\frac{15}{x^{1.5}} = -\frac{15}{\sqrt{x^3}} \end{aligned} \]

4The values

\[ Q'(25) = \frac{30}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{30}{5} = 6 \] \[ \begin{aligned} Q''(25) &= -\frac{15}{\sqrt{25^3}} = -\frac{15}{\sqrt{15625}} \\ &= -\frac{15}{125} = -0.12 \end{aligned} \]
Answer The marginal product of labour is 6 units an hour per worker, and it is changing by \(-0.12\) units an hour per worker for each further worker. If one more worker is taken on when 25 are employed, the output will grow by approximately 6 units an hour. If one more worker is taken on after that, the gain will be approximately 0.12 units an hour smaller. So every new worker still adds output, but adds less than the worker hired before.
Example 3

Resale value of a machine

Problem A machine’s resale value \(t\) years after it was bought is \[ V(t) = 40000e^{-0.25t} \] euros. Find the rate at which the value is changing four years after the purchase, and the rate at which that rate is changing. Interpret both results.

1The first derivative

The exponent is not simply \(t\), so the chain rule is needed. The outer function is the exponential \(e^u\) and the inner function is \(u(t) = -0.25t\). The rule for an exponential with an inner function is

\[ \big(e^{u(t)}\big)' = e^{u(t)}\cdot u'(t) \]

Here \(u'(t) = -0.25\), and the constant factor 40000 is simply carried along:

\[ \begin{aligned} V'(t) &= 40000\cdot e^{-0.25t}\cdot(-0.25) \\ &= -10000e^{-0.25t} \end{aligned} \]

2The second derivative

The first derivative has the same shape as the function itself, only with a different constant in front, so exactly the same rule is used once more:

\[ \begin{aligned} V''(t) &= -10000\cdot e^{-0.25t}\cdot(-0.25) \\ &= 2500e^{-0.25t} \end{aligned} \]

3The values

At \(t = 4\) the exponent is \(-0.25\cdot 4 = -1\):

\[ V'(4) = -10000e^{-1} \approx -3678.79 \] \[ V''(4) = 2500e^{-1} \approx 919.70 \]
Answer Four years after the purchase the machine is losing about 3678.79 euros of value a year, and that loss is becoming smaller by about 919.70 euros a year for each further year. If the machine is kept one year longer, it will lose approximately 3678.79 euros. The year after that it will lose approximately 919.70 euros less than this one. The two signs are different here, and both are needed: the value falls, but it falls more and more slowly.
Example 4

Users of a service

Problem The number of registered users of a service, in thousands, \(x\) months after its launch is \[ N(x) = 20\ln(2x + 1) \] Find the rate at which the number of users is growing two months after the launch, and the rate at which that growth is changing. Interpret both results.

1The first derivative

The chain rule is used again. The outer function is the logarithm \(\ln u\) and the inner function is \(u(x) = 2x + 1\). The rule for a logarithm with an inner function is

\[ \big(\ln u(x)\big)' = \frac{u'(x)}{u(x)} \]

Here \(u'(x) = 2\), so

\[ N'(x) = 20\cdot\frac{2}{2x + 1} = \frac{40}{2x + 1} \]

2The second derivative

The first derivative is a fraction with a constant numerator, so instead of the quotient rule it is easier to write it as a power, \(\dfrac{40}{2x + 1} = 40(2x + 1)^{-1}\), and use the power rule with an inner function:

\[ \big((u(x))^n\big)' = n(u(x))^{n-1}\cdot u'(x) \]

With \(n = -1\) and \(u(x) = 2x + 1\), \(u'(x) = 2\):

\[ \begin{aligned} N''(x) &= 40\cdot(-1)(2x + 1)^{-2}\cdot 2 \\ &= -\frac{80}{(2x + 1)^2} \end{aligned} \]

3The values

At \(x = 2\) the inner function is \(2\cdot 2 + 1 = 5\):

\[ N'(2) = \frac{40}{5} = 8 \] \[ N''(2) = -\frac{80}{5^2} = -\frac{80}{25} = -3.2 \]
Answer Two months after the launch the service is gaining 8 thousand users a month, and that gain is becoming smaller by 3.2 thousand a month for each further month. If one more month passes, the service will gain approximately 8 thousand users. The month after that it will gain approximately 3.2 thousand fewer new users than this one. The service keeps growing, but the number of new users each month is falling quickly.
Example 5

Value of the timber on a plantation

Problem The value of the standing timber on a plantation, in thousands of euros, \(t\) years from now is \[ W(t) = 50te^{-0.1t} + 200 \] Find the rate at which the value is changing five years from now, and the rate at which that rate is changing. Interpret both results.

1The first derivative

The first term is a product of two functions, so the product rule is used:

\[ \big(u(t)\cdot v(t)\big)' = u'(t)\cdot v(t) + u(t)\cdot v'(t) \]

Take \(u(t) = 50t\) and \(v(t) = e^{-0.1t}\). Then \(u'(t) = 50\), and the derivative of the second factor is found by the exponential form of the chain rule, whose inner function here is \(-0.1t\):

\[ v'(t) = e^{-0.1t}\cdot(-0.1) = -0.1e^{-0.1t} \]

The derivative of the constant 200 is zero, so

\[ \begin{aligned} W'(t) &= 50e^{-0.1t} + 50t\cdot\left(-0.1e^{-0.1t}\right) \\ &= 50e^{-0.1t} - 5te^{-0.1t} \\ &= 50e^{-0.1t}(1 - 0.1t) \end{aligned} \]

2The second derivative

The simplified first derivative is again a product, now of \(u(t) = 50e^{-0.1t}\) and \(v(t) = 1 - 0.1t\). Here \(u'(t) = -5e^{-0.1t}\) and \(v'(t) = -0.1\):

\[ \begin{aligned} W''(t) &= -5e^{-0.1t}(1 - 0.1t) \\ &\phantom{=}\; + 50e^{-0.1t}\cdot(-0.1) \end{aligned} \]

Both terms contain the factor \(e^{-0.1t}\), so it is taken outside the brackets:

\[ \begin{aligned} W''(t) &= e^{-0.1t}\left[-5(1 - 0.1t) - 5\right] \\ &= e^{-0.1t}(0.5t - 10) \end{aligned} \]

3The values

At \(t = 5\) the exponent is \(-0.5\):

\[ \begin{aligned} W'(5) &= 50e^{-0.5}\cdot(1 - 0.5) \\ &= 25e^{-0.5} \approx 15.16 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} W''(5) &= e^{-0.5}\cdot(2.5 - 10) \\ &= -7.5e^{-0.5} \approx -4.55 \end{aligned} \]
Answer Five years from now the timber is gaining about 15.16 thousand euros of value a year, and that gain is becoming smaller by about 4.55 thousand euros a year for each further year. If the plantation is left standing one more year, its value will grow by approximately 15.16 thousand euros. The year after that it will grow by approximately 4.55 thousand euros less. So the timber is still worth more each year, but each extra year adds less than the year before.
Example 6

Market share of a product

Problem The share of a market held by a product, in percent, \(t\) months after its launch is \[ S(t) = \frac{60t}{t + 10} \] Find the share, the rate at which it is growing and the rate at which that growth is changing ten months after the launch. Interpret the last two results.

1The first derivative

The function is a fraction, so the quotient rule is used:

\[ \left(\frac{u(t)}{v(t)}\right)' = \frac{u'(t)v(t) - u(t)v'(t)}{v^2(t)} \]

Label the numerator \(u(t) = 60t\) and the denominator \(v(t) = t + 10\), so \(u'(t) = 60\) and \(v'(t) = 1\):

\[ \begin{aligned} S'(t) &= \frac{60(t + 10) - 60t\cdot 1}{(t + 10)^2} \\ &= \frac{60t + 600 - 60t}{(t + 10)^2} = \frac{600}{(t + 10)^2} \end{aligned} \]

2The second derivative

The quotient rule is not needed a second time. The numerator is now a constant, so the first derivative is a single power with an inner function, \(\dfrac{600}{(t + 10)^2} = 600(t + 10)^{-2}\), and the power rule with an inner function applies, with \(u(t) = t + 10\) and \(u'(t) = 1\):

\[ \begin{aligned} S''(t) &= 600\cdot(-2)(t + 10)^{-3}\cdot 1 \\ &= -\frac{1200}{(t + 10)^3} \end{aligned} \]

3The values

At \(t = 10\) the inner function is \(t + 10 = 20\):

\[ S(10) = \frac{600}{20} = 30 \] \[ S'(10) = \frac{600}{20^2} = \frac{600}{400} = 1.5 \] \[ S''(10) = -\frac{1200}{20^3} = -\frac{1200}{8000} = -0.15 \]
Answer Ten months after the launch the product holds 30 percent of the market. The share is growing by 1.5 percentage points a month, and that growth is becoming smaller by 0.15 percentage points a month for each further month. If one more month passes, the share will rise by approximately 1.5 percentage points. The month after that it will rise by approximately 0.15 percentage points less. So the product still takes market share from its competitors, but it takes less and less of it every month.

Practice problems

Write the solutions out as in the examples: find both derivatives, calculate their values and interpret both results. The answer gives the derivatives, their values and the interpretation – use them to check your work.

The daily total cost of producing \(x\) units of a product is

\[ TC(x) = 0.02x^3 - 1.5x^2 + 60x + 800 \]

euros. Find \(TC'(20)\) and \(TC''(20)\) and interpret both results.

The derivatives:

\[ TC'(x) = 0.06x^2 - 3x + 60 \] \[ TC''(x) = 0.12x - 3 \]

The values:

\[ TC'(20) = 24;\qquad TC''(20) = -0.6 \]

Answer. At an output of 20 units a day one more unit adds about 24 euros to the cost, and that addition is falling by 0.6 euros for each further unit.

The stock of a product in a warehouse, in tonnes, \(t\) days after a delivery is

\[ Q(t) = 800e^{-0.1t} \]

Find \(Q'(10)\) and \(Q''(10)\) and interpret both results.

The derivatives (the chain rule):

\[ Q'(t) = -80e^{-0.1t} \] \[ Q''(t) = 8e^{-0.1t} \]

The values:

\[ Q'(10) = -80e^{-1} \approx -29.43 \] \[ Q''(10) = 8e^{-1} \approx 2.94 \]

Answer. Ten days after the delivery, the stock is falling by about 29.43 tonnes a day, and that fall is becoming smaller by about 2.94 tonnes a day every day.

A firm’s monthly sales, in thousands of units, \(t\) months after a redesign of its product are

\[ S(t) = 18\ln(t + 1) + 4t \]

Find \(S'(5)\) and \(S''(5)\) and interpret both results.

The derivatives (the chain rule in the first one):

\[ S'(t) = \frac{18}{t + 1} + 4 \] \[ S''(t) = -\frac{18}{(t + 1)^2} \]

The values:

\[ S'(5) = 7;\qquad S''(5) = -0.5 \]

Answer. Five months after the redesign, the sales are growing by 7 thousand units a month, and that growth is falling by 0.5 thousand units a month every month.

The share of customers, in percent, who pay with a new method \(t\) months after it was introduced is

\[ H(t) = \frac{80t}{t + 12} \]

Find \(H'(8)\) and \(H''(8)\) and interpret both results.

The derivatives (the quotient rule):

\[ H'(t) = \frac{960}{(t + 12)^2} \] \[ H''(t) = -\frac{1920}{(t + 12)^3} \]

The values:

\[ H'(8) = 2.4;\qquad H''(8) = -0.24 \]

Answer. Eight months after the method was introduced, the share is growing by 2.4 percentage points a month, and that growth is falling by 0.24 percentage points a month every month.
