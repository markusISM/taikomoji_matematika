Guidelines for Presenting Solutions to Applied Mathematics Problems

Each requirement is accompanied by a short example from this website showing how to meet it. The link below the example leads to the full solution. On a phone, wide tables can be scrolled sideways.

1Linear Functions and Models5 items

  1. When constructing a linear model (for example, a model of linear depreciation, a profit function, or break-even analysis), it is required to state what data are given in the problem.

    Example

    In the dough mixer problem, all the given data are written down before any calculation:

    Initial value: \(V(0) = 24000\).
    Expected usage time: \(T = 8\).
    Scrap value: \(V(8) = 4000\).

    Full example →

  2. When introducing new variables not specified in lectures or the problem, or when changing measurement units, this must be explicitly written in the solution.

    Example

    When a new variable is introduced in the solution, its meaning is written in a sentence:

    Let \(x\) be the number of units produced and sold per month. Then the costs of the two machines are:

    \[ \begin{aligned} TC_{\mathrm{I}} &= 21000 + 18x \\ TC_{\mathrm{II}} &= 15000 + 28x \end{aligned} \]

    Full example →

  3. If the problem includes several questions, answers to all of them must be clearly presented.

    Example

    The dough mixer problem includes three questions, so the answer contains three answers:

    Answer The rate of depreciation is 2500 euros per year; the book value at the end of year \(t\) is \(V(t) = 24000 - 2500t\); at the end of the third year the mixer is worth 16500 euros.

    Full example →

  4. When solving problems involving finding demand/supply or other linear functions, it is necessary to indicate the points or other information used to determine the parameters.

    Example

    In the electric scooter problem, the data used are indicated before the parameters are computed:

    Demand. The quantity is the first coordinate and the price is the second: \((x_1;\,p_1) = (2000;\,480)\), \((x_2;\,p_2) = (5000;\,360)\).

    Supply. Nothing is offered at the price of 160 euros, so the line passes through the point \((x_1;\,p_1) = (0;\,160)\). An increase of 60 euros in the price brings 1000 more units to the market: \(\Delta p = 60\), \(\Delta x = 1000\).

    Full example →

  5. When solving the problem of the choice of the means of production, the meaning of the introduced variable must be explained. A graph must be included in the solution.

    Example

    The meaning of the variable is written together with the cost functions:

    With \(x\) the number of parts required:

    \[ \begin{aligned} TC_a &= 230x \\ TC_b &= 70000 + 90x \\ TC_c &= 250000 + 10x \end{aligned} \]

    The graph shows the axis labels, the origin of the coordinate system, the unit scale on the axes and the labels of the lines:

    1. 1Axis labels: \(x\) – quantity (units), \(TC\) – total cost (EUR).
    2. 2The origin of the coordinate system.
    3. 3The unit scale on the axes: one division is 500 units and 100000 EUR.
    4. 4Labels of the lines: \(TC_a\), \(TC_b\), \(TC_c\).

    More about the graph →

2Matrices4 items

  1. When performing addition, subtraction, or scalar multiplication of matrices, it is sufficient to show the operation performed using the symbols given in the problem or introduced in the solution, and to present the resulting matrix. It is not necessary to rewrite the matrices in full or to show all arithmetic operations step by step.

    Example

    It is sufficient to write the operation using the symbols of the matrices and to present the resulting matrix – the matrices \(R\) and \(S\) themselves need not be rewritten:

    \[ R + S = \begin{pmatrix} 80 & 50 & 30 \\ 60 & 100 & 30 \end{pmatrix} \]

    Full example →

  2. When performing matrix multiplication, it is required to indicate the matrices being multiplied, their dimensions, and the structure of the resulting matrix. It is not necessary to rewrite all matrices or to show every arithmetic calculation.

    Example

    The matrices being multiplied, their dimensions and the structure of the resulting matrix are indicated:

    \[ (4 \times \htmlClass{hd}{3})\cdot(\htmlClass{hd}{3} \times 2) = 4 \times 2 \]

    The product \(C = A\cdot B\) has four rows (the departments) and two columns (the suppliers):

    \[ C = A\cdot B = \begin{pmatrix} c_{11} & c_{12} \\ c_{21} & c_{22} \\ c_{31} & c_{32} \\ c_{41} & c_{42} \end{pmatrix} \]

    The entry \(c_{i1}\) is the expense of department no. \(i\) when purchasing from Supplier I, and \(c_{i2}\) when purchasing from Supplier II.

    Full example →

  3. When solving market share prediction problems, the transition matrix \(P\) and the initial state probability vector \(X_0\) must be presented. When calculating the predicted market distribution for subsequent periods, the matrix operation performed must be clearly written.

    Example

    The initial state probability vector \(X_0\), the transition matrix \(P\) and the matrix operation performed are presented:

    \(X_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0.4 \\ 0.2 \end{pmatrix}\)\(P = \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 0.1 & 0.1 \\ 0.1 & 0.85 & 0.1 \\ 0.1 & 0.05 & 0.8 \end{pmatrix}\)
    \[ X_1 = P\cdot X_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0.38 \\ 0.4 \\ 0.22 \end{pmatrix} \]

    Full example →

  4. Do not forget to provide an interpretation of the result if the problem requires it. The interpretation must explain the meaning of the entries of the resulting matrix.

    Example

    The interpretation explains the meaning of the entries of the resulting matrix:

    The rows of the answer are the four departments and the columns are the two suppliers, so each entry is the amount in euros that one department would spend at one supplier. Department no. 1, for example, would spend 61 euros at Supplier I and 68.80 euros at Supplier II.

    Full example →

3Systems of Linear Equations7 items

  1. When solving applied systems of linear equations problems, the definitions of the introduced variables must be provided at the beginning of the solution. Each variable definition must clearly state that the variable represents the quantity of a specific object.

    Example

    • ✓“Let \(x\) be the number of type-A products made per day.”
    • ✓“Let \(y\) be the amount invested in bonds, in euros.”
    • ✗“\(x\) – type A.” It is not clear what \(x\) counts: products, minutes or euros.

    More →

  2. When solving steady-state market shares problems, it is required to indicate that the variables represent the steady-state market shares. The transition matrix \(P\) must also be provided.

    Example

    In a steady-state market shares problem:

    Let \(X = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix}\) be the vector of the steady-state market shares of suppliers A, B and C.

    \[ P = \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 0.1 & 0.1 \\ 0.1 & 0.85 & 0.1 \\ 0.1 & 0.05 & 0.8 \end{pmatrix} \]

    Full example →

  3. If convenient for the author, the data from the problem may be summarized in a table, but this is optional.

    Example

    For example, the data of the phone case problem in a table:

    Type-AType-BType-CTotal, min
    Machine I112240
    Machine II122300
    Machine III211180

    Full example →

  4. The solution must include the mathematical model of the problem – the system of linear equations. Presenting only the augmented matrix is not sufficient.

    Example

    • ✓The system of linear equations: \(\begin{cases} x + y + 2z = 240 \\ x + 2y + 2z = 300 \\ 2x + y + z = 180 \end{cases}\)
    • ✗Only the augmented matrix \(\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 240 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 & 300 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 & 180 \end{array}\right)\) – not sufficient.

    Full example →

  5. The system of equations must be solved using the Gaussian elimination method. The solution must show which row operations are performed to eliminate elements below the main diagonal.

    Example

    The first entry is already 1, so the first column is cleared straight away: the first row is multiplied by \(-1\) and added to the second row, and by \(-2\) and added to the third:

    \(\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 1 & 2 & 240 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 & 300 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 & 180 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{cc} \htmlClass{ha}{(-1)} & \htmlClass{ha}{(-2)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} & \htmlClass{ha}{\vert} \\ & \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\) \(\Longrightarrow\) \(\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 2 & 240 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & 1 & 0 & 60 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & -1 & -3 & -300 \end{array}\right)\)

    Full example →

  6. At the end of the solution, the final answer must be clearly presented.

    Example

    Answer

    The factory should make 20 type-A cases, 60 type-B cases and 80 type-C cases a day.

    This is the only production plan that uses all of the available machine time.

    Full example →

  7. If the system has infinitely many solutions, they must be expressed using a parameter. In applied problems, it is necessary to indicate the interval of possible parameter values. If required by the problem, particular solutions must also be provided.

    Example

    In the freezer purchase problem, the solutions are expressed using the parameter \(t\), and the interval of its possible values is indicated:

    \[ \begin{gathered} (x;\, y;\, z) = (8 + 2t;\; 12 - 3t;\; t) \\ t \in [0,\, 4] \cap \mathbb{Z} \end{gathered} \]

    A particular solution, for example for \(t = 2\): \((12;\, 6;\, 2)\).

    Full example →

4Linear Programming: Graphical Method8 items

  1. When solving applied linear programming problems, the definitions of the introduced variables must be provided at the beginning of the solution. Each variable definition must clearly state that the variable represents the quantity of a specific object.

    Example

    • ✓“\(x\) – the number of tables produced per week.”
    • ✗“\(x\) – tables.” It is not clear what \(x\) counts or over what period.

    More →

  2. If convenient for the author, the data from the problem may be summarized in a table, but this is optional.

    Example

    For example, the data of the cosmetics workshop problem in a table:

    Shower gelShampooAvailable
    Mixing, h119
    Filling, h3121
    Packing, h1214
    Profit, EUR5040—

    Full example →

  3. The solution must include the mathematical model of the problem – the objective function and the system of constraints. It must be clearly stated whether the objective function is to be maximized or minimized. All constraints, including the non-negativity constraints, must be clearly written together in one system, next to the objective function.

    Example

    The objective function with the direction \(\to \max\) stated and, next to it, all the constraints, including \(x;\, y \geq 0\), in one system:

    \[ \begin{gathered} P = 50x + 40y \to \max \\[4pt] \begin{cases} x + y \leq 9 \\ 3x + y \leq 21 \\ x + 2y \leq 14 \\ x;\, y \geq 0 \end{cases} \end{gathered} \]

    Full example →

  4. When solving the problem graphically, value tables for the constraint lines must be provided.

    Example

    A value table with two points for each constraint line:

    \(x + y = 9\)

    \(x = 0:\; y = 9\)
    \(y = 0:\; x = 9\)

    \(x\)09
    \(y\)90

    \(3x + y = 21\)

    \(x = 0:\; y = 21\)
    \(y = 0:\; 3x = 21 \Rightarrow x = 7\)

    \(x\)07
    \(y\)210

    \(x + 2y = 14\)

    \(x = 0:\; 2y = 14 \Rightarrow y = 7\)
    \(y = 0:\; x = 14\)

    \(x\)014
    \(y\)70

    Full example →

  5. The graph must include the following elements: axis labels; the origin of the coordinate system; the unit scale on the axes; labels of the lines; the feasible set.

    Example

    1. 1Axis labels: \(x\) and \(y\).
    2. 2The origin of the coordinate system, \(O\).
    3. 3The unit scale on the axes: the length of one division is marked on both axes.
    4. 4Labels of the lines: the equation of each line is written next to it, for example \(2x + y = 12\), or the line is otherwise clearly linked to its equation.
    5. 5The feasible set: the region is clearly marked. In this figure it is shaded – the polygon \(OABCD\) bounded by the lines and the coordinate axes.

    More about the graph →

  6. The coordinates of the intersection points of the constraint lines must be determined algebraically. When finding intersection points, it is acceptable to use a calculator, but the equations used must be shown explicitly.

    Example

    Both equations used to find the intersection point are shown:

    \(B\) is the intersection point of the lines \(x + y = 9\) and \(x + 2y = 14\): \[ \begin{gathered} \begin{cases} x + y = 9 \\ x + 2y = 14 \end{cases} \\ \Longrightarrow\; x = 4,\; y = 5 \;\Longrightarrow\; B(4;\, 5) \end{gathered} \]

    Subtracting the first equation from the second gives \(y = 5\); then \(x = 9 - 5 = 4\).

    Full example →

  7. The objective function value must be calculated at all vertices of the feasible set, except for the origin. This step must be clearly shown in the mathematically correct manner.

    Example

    The coordinates of every vertex except the origin are substituted into the objective function \(P = 50x + 40y\):

    \[ \begin{aligned} P_A &= 50\cdot 0 + 40\cdot 7 = 280 \\ P_B &= 50\cdot 4 + 40\cdot 5 \\ &= 200 + 200 = 400 \\ P_C &= 50\cdot 6 + 40\cdot 3 \\ &= 300 + 120 = 420 \\ P_D &= 50\cdot 7 + 40\cdot 0 = 350 \end{aligned} \]

    At the origin nothing is produced, so \(P_O = 0\) – this value need not be calculated.

    Full example →

  8. At the end of the solution, the final answer must be clearly formulated.

    Example

    Answer

    The workshop should produce 6 batches of shower gel and 3 batches of shampoo. The maximum profit is \(P_{\max}(6;\, 3) = 420\) euros.

    This plan uses \(6 + 3 = 9\) hours of mixing and \(3\cdot 6 + 3 = 21\) hours of filling – both completely – while packing is used only in the amount \(6 + 2\cdot 3 = 12\) of the 14 hours available. Mixing and filling are the scarce processes here; two hours of packing time remain idle.

    Full example →

5Linear Programming: Simplex Method8 items

  1. When solving applied linear programming problems, the definitions of the introduced variables must be provided at the beginning of the solution. Each variable definition must clearly state that the variable represents the quantity of a specific object.

    Example

    • ✓“\(x\) – the number of jars produced.”
    • ✗“\(x\) – jars.” It is not clear what \(x\) counts or in what units.

    In the glassworks problem all three variables are defined as quantities:

    • \(x\)– the number of jars produced;
    • \(y\)– the number of vases produced;
    • \(z\)– the number of bowls produced.

    Full example →

  2. If convenient for the author, the data from the problem may be summarized in a table, but this is optional.

    Example

    For example, the data of the glassworks problem in a table:

    JarVaseBowlAvailable
    Melting, h221400
    Forming, h112600
    Annealing, h311300
    Profit, EUR103020—

    Full example →

  3. The solution must include the mathematical model of the problem – the objective function and the system of constraints. It is not sufficient to present only the simplex tableau. It must be clearly stated whether the objective function is to be maximized or minimized. All constraints, including non-negativity constraints, must be written clearly together in one system, next to the objective function.

    Example

    The model is written separately, before the simplex tableau: the objective function with \(\to \max\) and all the constraints, including the non-negativity constraints:

    \[ \begin{gathered} P = 10x + 30y + 20z \to \max \\[4pt] \begin{cases} 2x + 2y + z \leq 400 \\ x + y + 2z \leq 600 \\ 3x + y + z \leq 300 \\ x;\, y;\, z \geq 0 \end{cases} \end{gathered} \]

    Full example →

  4. It is not required to explain the meaning of slack variables or the conversion of inequalities into equations.

    Example

    After the mathematical model, the initial simplex tableau can be written straight away:

    \(x\) \(y\) \(z\) \(s_1\) \(s_2\) \(s_3\) \(P\) \(n\)
    2 2 1 1 0 0 0 400
    1 1 2 0 1 0 0 600
    3 1 1 0 0 1 0 300
    −10 −30 −20 0 0 0 1 0

    Full example →

  5. After formulating the initial simplex tableau, it is necessary to indicate the pivot column and pivot row. The ratio calculations used to select the pivot row must be shown.

    Example

    In the initial tableau the pivot column and the pivot row are marked, and the ratio calculations used to select the pivot row are written beside it:

    \(x\) \(y\) \(z\) \(s_1\) \(s_2\) \(s_3\) \(P\) \(n\)
    2 2 1 1 0 0 0 400 : 2 400 : 2 = 200
    1 1 2 0 1 0 0 600 600 : 1 = 600
    3 1 1 0 0 1 0 300 300 : 1 = 300
    −10 −30 −20 0 0 0 1 0
    • pivot column
    • pivot row
    • pivot element

    Full example →

  6. When solving a minimization problem, the transposition of the model data table must be shown.

    Example

    The model data table and its transpose:

    \(x\)\(y\)\(n\)
    \(s_1\)3140
    \(s_2\)1120
    \(s_3\)1250
    \(TC\)20003000
     \(\Longrightarrow\)
    \(s_1\)\(s_2\)\(s_3\)\(n\)
    \(x\)3112000
    \(y\)1123000
    \(P\)402050

    The first row \(s_1\) has become the first column \(s_1\).

    Full example →

  7. During the simplex method process, row operations to eliminate elements above and below the pivot element must be shown.

    Example

    In the previous tableau “: 2” is written beside the pivot row – it is divided by the pivot element. After the division, the multipliers are written beside it, and the arrows show the rows whose entries they turn into zeros:

    \(x\) \(y\) \(z\) \(s_1\) \(s_2\) \(s_3\) \(P\) \(n\)
    1 1 0.5 0.5 0 0 0 200 (−1) (30)
    1 1 2 0 1 0 0 600 ↓ │
    3 1 1 0 0 1 0 300 ↓ │
    −10 −30 −20 0 0 0 1 0 ↓

    Full example →

  8. At the end of the solution, answers to all problem questions must be clearly formulated.

    Example

    Answer

    a) The glassworks should produce no jars, 100 vases and 200 bowls; the maximum profit is \(P_{\max}(0;\, 100;\, 200) = 7000\) euros.

    b) 100 hours of the forming process remain unused; the melting and the annealing hours are used up completely.

    c) The shadow prices are 10 euros per hour for melting, 0 for forming and 10 euros per hour for annealing: one extra hour of melting or of annealing would add 10 euros to the profit, whereas extra forming time would add nothing, because forming time is already in surplus.

    Full example →
