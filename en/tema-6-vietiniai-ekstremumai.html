Relative extrema

Many management problems ask where a quantity reaches a high or low value: which output gives the greatest profit, which price gives the greatest revenue, or which period of ownership gives the lowest average cost. Relative extrema describe values that are highest or lowest compared with nearby values. Derivatives help us locate and classify these candidates.

A function \(f(x)\) has a relative (local) maximum at \(x_0\) if its value there is at least as large as its values at nearby points. It has a relative (local) minimum if its value there is at most as large as its values at nearby points. Together, these are called relative extrema.

The word relative means that we compare nearby values only. A relative maximum need not be the largest value on the whole domain, and a relative minimum need not be the smallest. A function may have several local peaks and valleys. Finding an absolute maximum or minimum requires comparison over the full relevant domain.

The location of an extremum and the function value

Distinguish the location from the function value. The location \(x_0\) tells us at which output, price or time an extremum occurs. The value \(f(x_0)\) gives the corresponding profit, revenue or cost. State both when the question asks for them. In this section, relative extrema refer to interior points; endpoints must be considered separately when finding absolute extrema.

Critical points and the first-derivative test

To find possible relative extrema, first locate the critical points. A number \(x_0\) in the domain is a critical point if

\[ \begin{gathered} f'(x_0) = 0 \quad \text{or} \\ f'(x_0) \text{ does not exist.} \end{gathered} \]

Being a critical point is necessary for an interior relative extremum, but it is not sufficient. A zero derivative may occur while a function continues increasing or decreasing on both sides. Critical points are therefore candidates that must be classified.

The first-derivative test compares the signs immediately to the left and right of a critical point. For a function continuous at that point:

  • if \(f'(x)\) changes from positive to negative at \(x_0\), then \(f(x_0)\) is a relative maximum;
  • if \(f'(x)\) changes from negative to positive at \(x_0\), then \(f(x_0)\) is a relative minimum;
  • if \(f'(x)\) keeps the same sign on both sides, there is no extremum at \(x_0\).

The sign chart summarises the test. An upward arrow followed by a downward arrow indicates a relative maximum; a downward arrow followed by an upward arrow indicates a relative minimum. If the arrows point in the same direction, the critical point does not give a relative extremum.

At \(x_1\) there is a relative maximum, at \(x_2\) a relative minimum, and at \(x_3\) there is no extremum: the derivative is positive on both sides of it.

How to solve

  1. Define the variable, the quantity being studied and their units. If needed, construct the function: revenue is price times quantity, \(R(x) = p\cdot x\); profit is \(P(x) = R(x) - TC(x)\); average cost is total cost divided by the relevant quantity. Treat the variable as continuous when applying derivatives.
  2. Differentiate the function. If the derivative is a polynomial, combine like terms and retain its standard polynomial form. Simplify other expressions as needed.
  3. Find the critical points: solve \(f'(x) = 0\) and check where the derivative does not exist while the function is defined. Solve any resulting quadratic equation using the discriminant. Discard solutions that make no sense in the context of the problem, such as a negative quantity or time.
  4. Mark the critical points on a number line (in applied problems, starting from 0). Choose a test value from each interval into which they divide the line and substitute it into the derivative. Use the signs on both sides to classify each critical point as a relative maximum, a relative minimum or neither.
  5. Evaluate the original function at each confirmed extremum. Give the location and function value with their units. If the question asks for the largest or smallest value over the full domain, also justify why the local result gives that overall value.

The sign chart

Write the derivative’s sign above each interval and an arrow below it to show whether the original function increases or decreases. Label the classification below each critical point. A zero above a dot means the derivative is zero there; no derivative value is shown where it does not exist. Classify the critical point from the signs on both sides.

In applied problems a quantity, a price or a time cannot be negative, so start the sign chart at 0 and leave its right end open. If the function is not defined at 0, for example when it divides by the variable, mark this point with a hollow dot.

!Common mistakes
  • A critical point is not yet an extremum. A zero derivative may occur while a function continues increasing or decreasing on both sides. Classify the point from the signs on both sides.
  • The location and the value. \(x_0\) tells at which output, price or time the extremum occurs, and \(f(x_0)\) gives the profit, revenue or cost itself. State both when the question asks for them.
  • Relative and absolute. A relative maximum need not be the largest value over the whole domain. If the largest or smallest value is asked for, justify it.
  • Solutions that make no sense. Discard solutions that give a negative quantity or time.
  • The revenue function. When the price depends on the quantity, substitute the whole price expression before multiplying by \(x\).

Examples

Try to solve the problem yourself first, then reveal the solution one step at a time.

Example 1

Ferry revenue

Problem The number of passengers \(x\) a ferry route carries a month and the ticket price are related by \[ p = 120 - 0.1x \] euros. How many passengers per month maximise revenue? Find the corresponding ticket price and monthly revenue.

1The quantity

Monthly revenue is the ticket price multiplied by the number of passengers. The price depends on \(x\), so substitute the price relation before multiplying:

\[ \begin{aligned} R(x) &= p\cdot x = (120 - 0.1x)x \\ &= 120x - 0.1x^2 \end{aligned} \]

Revenue is measured in euros per month.

2The derivative

Differentiate the linear and quadratic terms:

\[ R'(x) = 120 - 0.2x \]

3The critical point

The polynomial derivative exists everywhere. Set it equal to zero:

\[ \begin{aligned} &120 - 0.2x = 0 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& 0.2x = 120 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& x = 600 \end{aligned} \]

4The signs

Choose a value on each side of the critical point and substitute it into the derivative:

\[ \begin{gathered} R'(200) = 120 - 40 = 80 > 0 \\ R'(1000) = 120 - 200 = -80 < 0 \end{gathered} \]

5The test and the values

The derivative changes from positive to negative at \(x = 600\), so \(R(600)\) is a relative maximum. Revenue increases before this output and decreases afterwards, so this is also the largest revenue.

\[ \begin{aligned} R(600) &= 120\cdot 600 - 0.1\cdot 600^2 \\ &= 72000 - 36000 = 36000 \end{aligned} \] \[ p = 120 - 0.1\cdot 600 = 60 \]
Answer Maximum monthly revenue is 36 000 euros at 600 passengers per month and a ticket price of 60 euros.
Example 2

Revenue from backpacks

Problem The price at which \(x\) backpacks a month can be sold is \[ p = 90 - 2\sqrt{x} \] euros. Find the monthly sales quantity and unit price that maximise revenue, and calculate the maximum revenue.

1The quantity

Multiply the unit price by the monthly sales quantity. Rewrite the square-root term as a power to apply the power rule:

\[ \begin{aligned} R(x) &= p\cdot x = (90 - 2\sqrt{x})x \\ &= 90x - 2x\sqrt{x} = 90x - 2x^{\frac{3}{2}} \end{aligned} \]

2The derivative

Apply the constant multiple rule and the power rule to each term:

\[ R'(x) = 90 - 2\cdot\frac{3}{2}x^{\frac{1}{2}} = 90 - 3\sqrt{x} \]

3The critical point

Set the derivative equal to zero. Isolate the square root, then square both sides:

\[ \begin{aligned} &90 - 3\sqrt{x} = 0 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& \sqrt{x} = 30 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& x = 900 \end{aligned} \]

4The signs

Choose a value on each side of the critical point:

\[ \begin{gathered} R'(400) = 90 - 3\cdot 20 = 30 > 0 \\ R'(1600) = 90 - 3\cdot 40 = -30 < 0 \end{gathered} \]

5The test and the values

The derivative changes from positive to negative at \(x = 900\), so \(R(900)\) is a relative maximum. Revenue increases before this output and decreases afterwards, so this is also the largest revenue.

\[ \begin{aligned} R(900) &= 90\cdot 900 - 2\cdot 900\cdot 30 \\ &= 81000 - 54000 = 27000 \end{aligned} \] \[ p = 90 - 2\cdot 30 = 30 \]
Answer Maximum monthly revenue is 27 000 euros at 900 backpacks per month and a unit price of 30 euros.
Example 3

Profit of a print shop

Problem A print shop sells \(x\) copies of a book a month at the price \[ p = -0.002x + 30 \] euros, and its monthly total cost is \[ TC(x) = 0.001x^2 + 6x + 4000 \] euros. Find the monthly sales quantity and unit price that maximise profit, and calculate the maximum profit.

1The quantity

First multiply quantity by unit price to obtain revenue. Then subtract the entire total cost function, changing the sign of each cost term, and combine like terms:

\[ \begin{aligned} R(x) &= p\cdot x = (-0.002x + 30)x \\ &= -0.002x^2 + 30x \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} P(x) &= R(x) - TC(x) \\ &= -0.002x^2 + 30x \\ &\phantom{=}\; - 0.001x^2 - 6x - 4000 \\ &= -0.003x^2 + 24x - 4000 \end{aligned} \]

2The derivative

Differentiate the simplified profit function term by term:

\[ P'(x) = -0.006x + 24 \]

3The critical point

Set the derivative equal to zero and solve the linear equation:

\[ \begin{aligned} &-0.006x + 24 = 0 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& 0.006x = 24 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& x = \frac{24}{0.006} = 4000 \end{aligned} \]

4The signs

Substitute one value below the critical point and one above it:

\[ \begin{gathered} P'(2000) = -12 + 24 = 12 > 0 \\ P'(6000) = -36 + 24 = -12 < 0 \end{gathered} \]

5The test and the values

The derivative changes from positive to negative at \(x = 4000\), giving a relative maximum. Profit increases before this output and decreases afterwards, so this is also the largest profit.

\[ \begin{aligned} P(4000) &= -0.003\cdot 4000^2 \\ &\phantom{=}\; + 24\cdot 4000 - 4000 \\ &= -48000 + 96000 - 4000 \\ &= 44000 \end{aligned} \] \[ p = -0.002\cdot 4000 + 30 = 22 \]
Answer Maximum monthly profit is 44 000 euros at 4000 copies per month and a unit price of 22 euros.
Example 4

Monthly profit from a new product

Problem A company’s monthly profit from a product, in thousands of euros, \(t\) months after its launch is \[ P(t) = t^3 - 18t^2 + 96t + 40 \] Find the locations and values of the relative extrema of profit.

1The derivative

Differentiate each term and keep the derivative in standard polynomial form:

\[ P'(t) = 3t^2 - 36t + 96 \]

2The critical points

The derivative exists everywhere. Set it equal to zero and divide by three. Solve the resulting quadratic using its discriminant:

\[ t^2 - 12t + 32 = 0 \] \[ \begin{aligned} D &= (-12)^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot 32 \\ &= 144 - 128 = 16 > 0 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{gathered} t_{1.2} = \frac{12 \pm \sqrt{16}}{2} = \frac{12 \pm 4}{2} \\ \Longrightarrow\; t_1 = 4;\quad t_2 = 8 \end{gathered} \]

There are two critical points, so both must be classified.

3The signs

Choose one interior test value from each of the three intervals and substitute it into the polynomial derivative:

\[ \begin{aligned} P'(2) &= 3\cdot 2^2 - 36\cdot 2 + 96 = 36 > 0 \\ P'(6) &= 3\cdot 6^2 - 36\cdot 6 + 96 = -12 < 0 \\ P'(10) &= 3\cdot 10^2 - 36\cdot 10 + 96 = 36 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

4The test and the values

At \(t = 4\), the derivative changes from positive to negative, giving a relative maximum. At \(t = 8\), it changes from negative to positive, giving a relative minimum. Substitute both times into the original profit function:

\[ \begin{gathered} P(4) = 64 - 288 + 384 + 40 = 200 \\ P(8) = 512 - 1152 + 768 + 40 = 168 \end{gathered} \]
Answer Relative maximum: 200 thousand euros at month four. Relative minimum: 168 thousand euros at month eight.
Example 5

Average annual cost of a delivery van

Problem A delivery van costs 21 600 euros to buy, and over its first \(t\) years repairs and servicing cost \(600t^2\) euros in total. The average annual cost of owning the van is therefore \[ A(t) = \frac{21600 + 600t^2}{t} \] euros per year. Find the period of ownership that minimises average annual cost, and calculate that minimum cost.

1The derivative

Divide each term in the numerator by time, then rewrite the reciprocal term as a negative power. This allows the power rule to be used directly:

\[ A(t) = \frac{21600}{t} + 600t = 21600t^{-1} + 600t \] \[ \begin{aligned} A'(t) &= -21600t^{-2} + 600 \\ &= 600 - \frac{21600}{t^2} \end{aligned} \]

2The critical point

For \(t > 0\), the derivative exists everywhere. Set it equal to zero, clear the nonzero denominator, and solve the resulting quadratic using its discriminant:

\[ 600 - \frac{21600}{t^2} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; t^2 - 36 = 0 \] \[ D = 0^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot(-36) = 144 > 0 \] \[ \begin{gathered} t_{1.2} = \frac{-0 \pm \sqrt{144}}{2} = \pm 6 \\ \Longrightarrow\; t_1 = -6;\quad t_2 = 6 \end{gathered} \]

Discard \(t = -6\) because the period of ownership cannot be negative. Only the positive root is a critical point.

3The signs

Substitute one positive time below the critical point and one above it. The function is not defined at \(t = 0\), so this point is marked with a hollow dot in the chart:

\[ \begin{gathered} A'(3) = 600 - 2400 = -1800 < 0 \\ A'(12) = 600 - 150 = 450 > 0 \end{gathered} \]

4The test and the value

The derivative changes from negative to positive at \(t = 6\), giving a relative minimum. Average annual cost decreases before this time and increases afterwards, so this is also the lowest average annual cost.

\[ \begin{aligned} A(6) &= \frac{21600}{6} + 600\cdot 6 \\ &= 3600 + 3600 = 7200 \end{aligned} \]
Answer The minimum average annual cost is 7200 euros per year after six years of ownership.
Example 6

Market share after a rebranding

Problem \(t\) months after a rebranding a company holds \[ S(t) = \frac{400t}{t^2 + 64},\quad t \geq 0 \] percent of its market. Find when market share is largest and calculate that share.

1The derivative

Apply the quotient rule. Differentiate the numerator and denominator separately, retain the subtraction sign, and square the original denominator. Expand and combine the terms in the numerator:

\[ \begin{aligned} S'(t) &= \frac{400(t^2 + 64) - 400t\cdot 2t}{(t^2 + 64)^2} \\ &= \frac{400t^2 + 25600 - 800t^2}{(t^2 + 64)^2} \\ &= \frac{-400t^2 + 25600}{(t^2 + 64)^2} \end{aligned} \]

2The critical point

The denominator is always positive, so the derivative exists everywhere. Set the numerator equal to zero, divide by its nonzero coefficient, and solve the quadratic using the discriminant:

\[ t^2 - 64 = 0 \] \[ D = 0^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot(-64) = 256 > 0 \] \[ \begin{gathered} t_{1.2} = \frac{-0 \pm \sqrt{256}}{2} = \pm 8 \\ \Longrightarrow\; t_1 = -8;\quad t_2 = 8 \end{gathered} \]

Discard \(t = -8\) because time is measured from the rebranding onwards.

3The signs

Choose a nonnegative time on each side of the critical point and substitute it into the derivative:

\[ S'(4) = \frac{-400\cdot 4^2 + 25600}{(4^2 + 64)^2} = 3 > 0 \]
\[ S'(12) = \frac{-400\cdot 12^2 + 25600}{(12^2 + 64)^2} \approx -0.74 < 0 \]

4The test and the value

The derivative changes from positive to negative at \(t = 8\), so \(S(8)\) is a relative maximum. Market share increases before this time and decreases afterwards, so this is also its largest value.

\[ S(8) = \frac{400\cdot 8}{64 + 64} = \frac{3200}{128} = 25 \]
Answer The maximum market share is 25 percent, reached eight months after the rebranding.
Example 7

Training time and worker output

Problem A firm gives each worker \(x\) hours of training a month. A trained worker then assembles \[ Q(x) = 400 + 300\ln(x + 1) - 50x \] units per month. Find the monthly training time that maximises output per worker and calculate the maximum output.

1The derivative

Use the chain rule for the logarithm: the inner function is \(u(x) = x + 1\), whose derivative is one. Combine the terms over a common denominator and collect the numerator:

\[ \begin{aligned} Q'(x) &= \frac{300}{x + 1} - 50 = \frac{300 - 50(x + 1)}{x + 1} \\ &= \frac{-50x + 250}{x + 1} \end{aligned} \]

2The critical point

Training time is nonnegative, so the denominator is nonzero and the derivative exists. Set the numerator equal to zero:

\[ -50x + 250 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 5 \]

3The signs

Substitute one training time below the critical point and one above it:

\[ \begin{gathered} Q'(1) = \frac{200}{2} = 100 > 0 \\ Q'(9) = \frac{-200}{10} = -20 < 0 \end{gathered} \]

4The test and the value

The derivative changes from positive to negative at \(x = 5\), so \(Q(5)\) is a relative maximum. Output increases before this training time and decreases afterwards, so this is also the largest output.

\[ Q(5) = 400 + 300\ln 6 - 50\cdot 5 \approx 687.5 \]
Answer Maximum output is approximately 687.5 units per worker per month, with five hours of training per month.
Example 8

Cumulative revenue without an extremum

Problem The revenue a software firm has earned from a product altogether, in thousands of euros, \(t\) years after its release is \[ R(t) = t^3 - 12t^2 + 48t + 100 \] Find any relative extrema of cumulative revenue.

1The derivative

Differentiate term by term and retain the standard polynomial form:

\[ R'(t) = 3t^2 - 24t + 48 \]

2The critical point

The derivative exists everywhere. Set it equal to zero and divide by three. Use the discriminant to solve the quadratic:

\[ t^2 - 8t + 16 = 0 \] \[ D = (-8)^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot 16 = 64 - 64 = 0 \] \[ t_{1.2} = \frac{8 \pm \sqrt{0}}{2} = 4 \]

A zero discriminant gives one repeated root. Its classification still depends on the derivative’s signs on both sides.

3The signs

Choose one value on each side of the critical point and substitute it into the polynomial derivative:

\[ \begin{aligned} R'(2) &= 3\cdot 2^2 - 24\cdot 2 + 48 = 12 > 0 \\ R'(6) &= 3\cdot 6^2 - 24\cdot 6 + 48 = 12 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

4The test

The derivative is positive on both sides of \(t = 4\), so this critical point gives neither a relative maximum nor a relative minimum.

Answer Cumulative revenue has no relative extrema: the sign of the derivative does not change at the critical point \(t = 4\).

Practice problems

Write the solutions out as in the examples: the quantity, the derivative, the critical points, the signs and the sign chart, the test and the values, the answer. The answer gives the functions, the derivatives and the final results – use them to check your work.

The weekly demand for a gadget is given by

\[ p = 200e^{-0.002x} \]

euros, where \(x\) is the number of gadgets sold. How many gadgets a week should be sold to earn the largest revenue, what price does that mean, and how large is the revenue?

The objective function:

\[ R(x) = p\cdot x = 200xe^{-0.002x} \]

The derivative (the product rule, with the chain rule inside):

\[ \begin{aligned} R'(x) &= 200e^{-0.002x} \\ &\phantom{=}\; + 200x\cdot e^{-0.002x}\cdot(-0.002) \\ &= 200e^{-0.002x}(1 - 0.002x) \end{aligned} \]

Answer. An exponential is never zero, so the critical point comes from \(1 - 0.002x = 0\), that is \(x = 500\). The derivative passes from plus to minus there, so the revenue has a maximum. Selling 500 gadgets a week at \(p = 200e^{-1} \approx 73.58\) euros gives the largest revenue, \(R(500) \approx 36787.94\) euros a week.

A workshop sells \(x\) units a month at the price

\[ p = 150 - 2\sqrt{x} \]

euros. The variable cost is 30 euros a unit and the fixed costs are 4000 euros a month. How many units should be made and sold to earn the largest profit, at what price, and how large is that profit?

The objective function:

\[ \begin{aligned} R(x) &= p\cdot x = 150x - 2x\sqrt{x} \\ &= 150x - 2x^{\frac{3}{2}} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P(x) &= R(x) - TC(x) \\ &= 150x - 2x^{\frac{3}{2}} - 30x - 4000 \\ &= 120x - 2x^{\frac{3}{2}} - 4000 \end{aligned} \]

The derivative:

\[ P'(x) = 120 - 3\sqrt{x} \]

Answer. The critical point is

\[ \begin{aligned} &120 - 3\sqrt{x} = 0 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& \sqrt{x} = 40 \\ \Longrightarrow\;& x = 1600 \end{aligned} \]

and the derivative passes from plus to minus there, so the profit has a maximum. Making and selling 1600 units a month at \(p = 70\) euros gives the largest profit, \(P(1600) = 60\,000\) euros a month.

A firm’s quarterly profit, in thousands of euros, \(t\) quarters after a reorganisation is

\[ P(t) = 2t^3 - 39t^2 + 180t + 300 \]

Find the relative extrema of the profit and interpret them.

The derivative:

\[ P'(t) = 6t^2 - 78t + 180 \]

The critical points:

\[ \begin{aligned} D &= (-78)^2 - 4\cdot 6\cdot 180 \\ &= 6084 - 4320 = 1764 \\ \sqrt{D} &= 42 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{gathered} t_1 = \frac{78 - 42}{2\cdot 6} = 3 \\ t_2 = \frac{78 + 42}{2\cdot 6} = 10 \end{gathered} \]

Answer. At \(t = 3\) the derivative passes from plus to minus, so \(P(3) = 543\) thousand euros is a relative maximum; at \(t = 10\) it passes from minus to plus, so \(P(10) = 200\) thousand euros is a relative minimum. The reorganisation lifts the profit for three quarters, the profit then slides for seven quarters, and from the tenth quarter on it grows again.

A workshop’s average cost of making one unit, in euros, when it makes \(x\) units a day is

\[ ATC(x) = \frac{x^2 + 40x + 4000}{x + 20} \]

At which output is the average cost smallest, and how large is it then?

The derivative (the quotient rule):

\[ \begin{gathered} ATC'(x) = \\ \frac{(2x + 40)(x + 20) - (x^2 + 40x + 4000)}{(x + 20)^2} \\ = \frac{x^2 + 40x - 3200}{(x + 20)^2} \end{gathered} \]

The critical points:

\[ \begin{aligned} D &= 40^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot(-3200) \\ &= 1600 + 12800 = 14400 \\ \sqrt{D} &= 120 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{gathered} x_1 = \frac{-40 + 120}{2} = 40 \\ x_2 = \frac{-40 - 120}{2} = -80 \end{gathered} \]

Answer. The root \(x_2 = -80\) is a negative output and is discarded. At \(x = 40\) the derivative passes from minus to plus, so the average cost has a minimum: making 40 units a day makes a unit cheapest, \(ATC(40) = 120\) euros.
