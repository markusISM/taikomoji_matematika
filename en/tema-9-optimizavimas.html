Standard Optimization Problems

This section looks for the maximum profit, the minimum cost and other optimal decisions when a quantity depends on two variables that are not connected by any additional condition. Most attention is paid to solving the system of equations of the necessary condition.

The objective function

In an applied problem the function whose largest or smallest value has to be found is called the objective function. It is often not given directly, so the first step of the solution is to build it. The most common objective functions are:

  • the profit of two products, \(P(x;\,y) = R(x;\,y) - TC(x;\,y)\), with the revenue \(R = p_1x + p_2y\) when the prices are fixed, or \(R = p_1(x;\,y)\cdot x + p_2(x;\,y)\cdot y\) when the prices are given by demand functions (the Topic 8 section “Marginal Analysis”);
  • profit built from a production function (the Topic 8 section “The Production Function”):
    \[ P(x;\,y) = p\cdot Q(x;\,y) - c_1x - c_2y - FC \]
    \[ \begin{aligned} P(x;\,y) &= p\cdot Q(x;\,y) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- c_1x - c_2y - FC \end{aligned} \]
  • profit as a function of the prices, when the quantities sold depend on the prices: each unit sold brings its price minus its unit cost;
  • any other quantity described by a function of two variables: a cost, a yield, a distance.

The objective function that has been built is optimized with the necessary and the sufficient conditions of an extremum, as explained on the Topic 9 page.

The system of the necessary condition

Setting both first order partial derivatives equal to zero gives a system of two equations with two unknowns. Its solution is the critical point. How the system is solved depends on whether it is linear.

A system of linear equations

When the objective function is a polynomial of the second degree, as in most problems of this section, both partial derivatives are linear. This gives a system of linear equations, which is most conveniently solved by substitution:

  1. Arrange the equations. If the equations contain decimals, multiply each equation by a number that makes all the coefficients integers. Every term of the equation is multiplied by the same number, including the constant term. Keep the variables on the left-hand side and move the numbers to the right-hand side. A term that is moved changes its sign.
  2. Express one variable. Choose an equation in which the coefficient of one of the variables is 1 or \(-1\), and express that variable from it. This avoids fractions.
  3. Substitute the expression into the other equation. Write the expression into the other equation in place of the variable that was expressed. If the variable has a coefficient or a minus sign in front of it, write the expression in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable.
  4. Solve the equation in one variable. Expand the brackets, collect like terms, move the numbers to the right-hand side and divide both sides by the coefficient of the variable.
  5. Find the second variable. Substitute the value found into the expression obtained in step 2.
  6. Check. Substitute the values of both variables into both equations – both must give true equalities.

A system of linear equations can also be solved by Gaussian elimination, which was discussed in Topic 3. Both methods give the same solution. In the examples the system is solved by substitution, and at the end of each solution there is a collapsible box “Solution by Gaussian elimination” for those who prefer this method.

A system of non-linear equations

If the objective function contains powers with fractional exponents, logarithms, exponential functions or fractions with the variable in the denominator, the partial derivatives are not linear, and a system of non-linear equations is obtained. Gaussian elimination cannot be used for it: it applies only to systems of linear equations, in which each unknown is of the first degree and is only multiplied by a number. Such systems are solved in other ways:

  • Cobb-Douglas functions. Move the costs of the inputs to the right-hand sides of the equations and divide the first equation by the second. After the powers of the same base are combined, express one variable through the other and substitute it into one of the equations (Example 8).
  • Fractions. Write the derivative as a single fraction. A fraction is equal to zero when its numerator is equal to zero and its denominator is not equal to zero (Example 9).
  • Exponential functions. Rearrange the equation into the form \(e^{u} = a\) and take the natural logarithm of both sides (Example 11).

Often each equation of such a system contains only one variable. Then the equations are solved separately.

How to solve

  1. Build the objective function.
  2. Find the first order partial derivatives, set them equal to zero and solve the system of equations (see “The system of the necessary condition”). If the equations contain decimals, first multiply the equations by a suitable number. If a derivative is a fraction, write it as a single fraction and replace the equation by two conditions: the numerator is equal to zero, and the denominator is not equal to zero.
  3. Label the critical point with a letter, for example \(K\). Find the second order partial derivatives and substitute the coordinates of \(K\) into them – this gives \(A\), \(B\), \(C\). Calculate \(D = AC - B^2\). Decide whether the point is a maximum or a minimum point (the second derivative test). If the profit function is built from a Cobb-Douglas production function, check the parameters (Cobb-Douglas functions).
  4. Calculate the quantities the problem asks for: the maximum or minimum value, prices, output.
  5. Write the answer in the words of the problem.
!Common mistakes
  • Not every term of the equation is multiplied. When an equation is multiplied or divided, all its terms, including the constant term, must be multiplied or divided by the same number.
  • The sign is not changed. A term moved to the other side of the equation changes its sign. When an equation is multiplied by a negative number, the signs of all its terms change.
  • Brackets are left out when substituting. If the variable has a coefficient or a minus sign in front of it, the substituted expression is written in brackets: \(2(600 - 3x)\), not \(2\cdot 600 - 3x\).
  • Gaussian elimination is applied to a non-linear system. Gaussian elimination is suitable only for systems of linear equations.
  • The sufficient condition is not checked. A critical point can be a minimum point, a maximum point or a saddle point.
  • The optimal value is calculated by substituting the coordinates into something other than the objective function. The maximum profit or the minimum cost is found by substituting the coordinates of the critical point into the objective function, not into its derivatives.

Revenue, cost and profit

Example 1

Office chairs and desks

Problem A furniture factory produces \(x\) office chairs and \(y\) office desks a month. Its monthly profit, in euros, is
\[ P(x;\,y) = -0.3x^2 - 0.2y^2 - 0.2xy + 120x + 100y - 5000 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P(x;\,y) &= -0.3x^2 - 0.2y^2 - 0.2xy \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ 120x + 100y - 5000 \end{aligned} \]
How many chairs and desks should the factory produce to maximize the profit? What is the maximum profit?

1The first order partial derivatives

The profit function is given directly, so we find the first order partial derivatives at once. When differentiating with respect to \(x\), the terms \(-0.2y^2\), \(100y\) and \(-5000\) are constants, and with respect to \(y\) – the terms \(-0.3x^2\), \(120x\) and \(-5000\):

\[ P'_x = -0.6x - 0.2y + 120;\qquad P'_y = -0.4y - 0.2x + 100 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P'_x &= -0.6x - 0.2y + 120 \\ P'_y &= -0.4y - 0.2x + 100 \end{aligned} \]

2The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -0.6x - 0.2y + 120 &= 0 \\ -0.2x - 0.4y + 100 &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

The equations contain decimals. To make the coefficients integers, we multiply each equation by \(-5\). Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} -0.6x - 0.2y + 120 = 0\;\big|\cdot (-5) \;&\Longrightarrow\; 3x + y - 600 = 0 \\ -0.2x - 0.4y + 100 = 0\;\big|\cdot (-5) \;&\Longrightarrow\; x + 2y - 500 = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} -0.6x - 0.2y + 120 = 0\;\big|\cdot (-5) \\ \Longrightarrow\; 3x + y - 600 = 0 \\[4pt] -0.2x - 0.4y + 100 = 0\;\big|\cdot (-5) \\ \Longrightarrow\; x + 2y - 500 = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 3x + y &= 600 \\ x + 2y &= 500 \end{aligned} \right. \]

3Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(y\) is 1, so it is most convenient to express \(y\) from it. We move the term \(3x\) to the right-hand side and change its sign:

\[ 3x + y = 600 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = 600 - 3x \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 3x + y = 600 \\ \Longrightarrow\; y = 600 - 3x \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). Since \(y\) is multiplied by 2 in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(x\):

\[ x + 2(600 - 3x) = 500 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ x + 1200 - 6x = 500 \;\Longrightarrow\; -5x = 500 - 1200 \;\Longrightarrow\; -5x = -700 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x + 1200 - 6x = 500 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -5x = 500 - 1200 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -5x = -700 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(-5\):

\[ x = \frac{-700}{-5} = 140 \]

We substitute the value of \(x\) into the expression \(y = 600 - 3x\):

\[ y = 600 - 3\cdot 140 = 600 - 420 = 180 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 600 - 3\cdot 140 \\ &= 600 - 420 = 180 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(140;\,180)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(3\cdot 140 + 180 = 600\) and \(140 + 2\cdot 180 = 500\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is 1, so we interchange the equations – this 1 becomes the pivot element:

\[ \left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 2 & 500 \\ 3 & 1 & 600 \end{array}\right) \]

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-3\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(-5\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 2 & 500 \\ 3 & 1 & 600 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-3)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 500 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & -5 & -900 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,(-5)} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 2 & 500 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 180 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 180\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x + 2\cdot 180 = 500 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 140 \]

The same solution \((140;\,180)\) is obtained.

4The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = P''_{xx}(K) = -0.6;\quad B = P''_{xy}(K) = -0.2;\quad C = P''_{yy}(K) = -0.4 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-0.6)\cdot (-0.4) - (-0.2)^2 = 0.24 - 0.04 = 0.2 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= P''_{xx}(K) = -0.6 \\ B &= P''_{xy}(K) = -0.2 \\ C &= P''_{yy}(K) = -0.4 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-0.6)\cdot (-0.4) - (-0.2)^2 \\ &= 0.24 - 0.04 = 0.2 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(140;\,180)\) is a maximum point.

5The maximum profit

We substitute the coordinates of the critical point into the profit function:

\[ P_{\max}(140;\,180) = -5880 - 6480 - 5040 + 16\,800 + 18\,000 - 5000 = 12\,400 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P_{\max}(140;\,180) \\ &= -5880 - 6480 - 5040 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ 16\,800 + 18\,000 - 5000 \\ &= 12\,400 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The factory should produce 140 chairs and 180 desks a month. The maximum profit is 12 400 euros a month.

Example 2

Garden benches and tables

Problem A workshop sells a garden bench at 180 euros and a garden table at 190 euros. When it produces \(x\) benches and \(y\) tables a month, its total cost, in euros, is
\[ TC(x;\,y) = 0.4x^2 + 0.3y^2 + 0.2xy + 20x + 40y + 3000 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} TC(x;\,y) &= 0.4x^2 + 0.3y^2 + 0.2xy \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ 20x + 40y + 3000 \end{aligned} \]
How many benches and tables should the workshop produce to maximize the profit? What is the maximum profit?

1The objective function

The prices are fixed, so the revenue is \(R(x;\,y) = 180x + 190y\). The profit is the revenue minus the cost. The cost function is written in brackets because all its terms are subtracted:

\[ \begin{aligned} P(x;\,y) &= 180x + 190y - (0.4x^2 + 0.3y^2 + 0.2xy + 20x + 40y + 3000) \\ &= 160x + 150y - 0.4x^2 - 0.3y^2 - 0.2xy - 3000 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P(x;\,y) \\ &= 180x + 190y - (0.4x^2 + 0.3y^2 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ 0.2xy + 20x + 40y + 3000) \\ &= 160x + 150y - 0.4x^2 - 0.3y^2 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 0.2xy - 3000 \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

\[ P'_x = 160 - 0.8x - 0.2y;\qquad P'_y = 150 - 0.6y - 0.2x \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P'_x &= 160 - 0.8x - 0.2y \\ P'_y &= 150 - 0.6y - 0.2x \end{aligned} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 160 - 0.8x - 0.2y &= 0 \\ 150 - 0.6y - 0.2x &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

The equations contain decimals. To make the coefficients integers, we multiply each equation by 5. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 160 - 0.8x - 0.2y = 0\;\big|\cdot 5 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 800 - 4x - y = 0 \\ 150 - 0.6y - 0.2x = 0\;\big|\cdot 5 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 750 - 3y - x = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 160 - 0.8x - 0.2y = 0\;\big|\cdot 5 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 800 - 4x - y = 0 \\[4pt] 150 - 0.6y - 0.2x = 0\;\big|\cdot 5 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 750 - 3y - x = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 4x + y &= 800 \\ x + 3y &= 750 \end{aligned} \right. \]

4Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(y\) is 1, so it is most convenient to express \(y\) from it. We move the term \(4x\) to the right-hand side and change its sign:

\[ 4x + y = 800 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = 800 - 4x \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 4x + y = 800 \\ \Longrightarrow\; y = 800 - 4x \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). Since \(y\) is multiplied by 3 in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(x\):

\[ x + 3(800 - 4x) = 750 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ x + 2400 - 12x = 750 \;\Longrightarrow\; -11x = 750 - 2400 \;\Longrightarrow\; -11x = -1650 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x + 2400 - 12x = 750 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -11x = 750 - 2400 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -11x = -1650 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(-11\):

\[ x = \frac{-1650}{-11} = 150 \]

We substitute the value of \(x\) into the expression \(y = 800 - 4x\):

\[ y = 800 - 4\cdot 150 = 800 - 600 = 200 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 800 - 4\cdot 150 \\ &= 800 - 600 = 200 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(150;\,200)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(4\cdot 150 + 200 = 800\) and \(150 + 3\cdot 200 = 750\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is 1, so we interchange the equations – this 1 becomes the pivot element:

\[ \left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 3 & 750 \\ 4 & 1 & 800 \end{array}\right) \]

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-4\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(-11\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 3 & 750 \\ 4 & 1 & 800 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-4)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & 750 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & -11 & -2200 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,(-11)} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & 750 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 200 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 200\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x + 3\cdot 200 = 750 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 150 \]

The same solution \((150;\,200)\) is obtained.

5The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = P''_{xx}(K) = -0.8;\quad B = P''_{xy}(K) = -0.2;\quad C = P''_{yy}(K) = -0.6 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-0.8)\cdot (-0.6) - (-0.2)^2 = 0.48 - 0.04 = 0.44 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= P''_{xx}(K) = -0.8 \\ B &= P''_{xy}(K) = -0.2 \\ C &= P''_{yy}(K) = -0.6 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-0.8)\cdot (-0.6) - (-0.2)^2 \\ &= 0.48 - 0.04 = 0.44 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(150;\,200)\) is a maximum point.

6The maximum profit

\[ P_{\max}(150;\,200) = 24\,000 + 30\,000 - 9000 - 12\,000 - 6000 - 3000 = 24\,000 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P_{\max}(150;\,200) \\ &= 24\,000 + 30\,000 - 9000 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 12\,000 - 6000 - 3000 \\ &= 24\,000 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The workshop should produce 150 benches and 200 tables a month. The maximum profit is 24 000 euros a month.

Example 3

Portable and home speakers

Problem A company produces \(x\) portable speakers and \(y\) home speakers a month. The prices, in euros, at which these quantities can be sold are
\[ p_1 = 90 - 0.02x - 0.01y;\qquad p_2 = 120 - 0.01x - 0.03y \]
\[ \begin{gathered} p_1 = 90 - 0.02x - 0.01y; \\ p_2 = 120 - 0.01x - 0.03y \end{gathered} \]
The variable cost of one speaker is 30 euros for a portable speaker and 40 euros for a home speaker. The fixed cost is 8000 euros a month.
a) How many speakers of each type should the company produce to maximize the profit?
b) At what prices should the speakers be sold? What is the maximum profit?

1The objective function

The price of each type of speaker is multiplied by the quantity of speakers of that type:

\[ \begin{aligned} R(x;\,y) &= (90 - 0.02x - 0.01y)x + (120 - 0.01x - 0.03y)y \\ &= 90x + 120y - 0.02x^2 - 0.03y^2 - 0.02xy \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} R(x;\,y) &= (90 - 0.02x - 0.01y)x \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ (120 - 0.01x - 0.03y)y \\ &= 90x + 120y - 0.02x^2 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 0.03y^2 - 0.02xy \end{aligned} \]

The cost is the sum of the variable and the fixed costs, and the profit is the revenue minus the cost:

\[ \begin{aligned} TC(x;\,y) &= 30x + 40y + 8000 \\ P(x;\,y) &= 60x + 80y - 0.02x^2 - 0.03y^2 - 0.02xy - 8000 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} TC(x;\,y) &= 30x + 40y + 8000 \\ P(x;\,y) &= 60x + 80y - 0.02x^2 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 0.03y^2 - 0.02xy - 8000 \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

\[ P'_x = 60 - 0.04x - 0.02y;\qquad P'_y = 80 - 0.06y - 0.02x \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P'_x &= 60 - 0.04x - 0.02y \\ P'_y &= 80 - 0.06y - 0.02x \end{aligned} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 60 - 0.04x - 0.02y &= 0 \\ 80 - 0.06y - 0.02x &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

The equations contain decimals. To make the coefficients integers, we multiply each equation by 50. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 60 - 0.04x - 0.02y = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 3000 - 2x - y = 0 \\ 80 - 0.06y - 0.02x = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 4000 - 3y - x = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 60 - 0.04x - 0.02y = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 3000 - 2x - y = 0 \\[4pt] 80 - 0.06y - 0.02x = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 4000 - 3y - x = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 2x + y &= 3000 \\ x + 3y &= 4000 \end{aligned} \right. \]

4Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(y\) is 1, so it is most convenient to express \(y\) from it. We move the term \(2x\) to the right-hand side and change its sign:

\[ 2x + y = 3000 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = 3000 - 2x \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 2x + y = 3000 \\ \Longrightarrow\; y = 3000 - 2x \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). Since \(y\) is multiplied by 3 in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(x\):

\[ x + 3(3000 - 2x) = 4000 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ x + 9000 - 6x = 4000 \;\Longrightarrow\; -5x = 4000 - 9000 \;\Longrightarrow\; -5x = -5000 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x + 9000 - 6x = 4000 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -5x = 4000 - 9000 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -5x = -5000 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(-5\):

\[ x = \frac{-5000}{-5} = 1000 \]

We substitute the value of \(x\) into the expression \(y = 3000 - 2x\):

\[ y = 3000 - 2\cdot 1000 = 3000 - 2000 = 1000 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 3000 - 2\cdot 1000 \\ &= 3000 - 2000 = 1000 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(1000;\,1000)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(2\cdot 1000 + 1000 = 3000\) and \(1000 + 3\cdot 1000 = 4000\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is 1, so we interchange the equations – this 1 becomes the pivot element:

\[ \left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 3 & 4000 \\ 2 & 1 & 3000 \end{array}\right) \]

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-2\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(-5\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 3 & 4000 \\ 2 & 1 & 3000 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-2)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & 4000 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & -5 & -5000 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,(-5)} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & 4000 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 1000 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 1000\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x + 3\cdot 1000 = 4000 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 1000 \]

The same solution \((1000;\,1000)\) is obtained.

5The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = P''_{xx}(K) = -0.04;\quad B = P''_{xy}(K) = -0.02;\quad C = P''_{yy}(K) = -0.06 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-0.04)\cdot (-0.06) - (-0.02)^2 = 0.0024 - 0.0004 = 0.002 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= P''_{xx}(K) = -0.04 \\ B &= P''_{xy}(K) = -0.02 \\ C &= P''_{yy}(K) = -0.06 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-0.04)\cdot (-0.06) - (-0.02)^2 \\ &= 0.0024 - 0.0004 = 0.002 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(1000;\,1000)\) is a maximum point.

6The prices and the maximum profit

We find the prices by substituting \(x = 1000\) and \(y = 1000\) into the price formulas, and the profit by substituting them into the profit function:

\[ p_1 = 90 - 20 - 10 = 60;\qquad p_2 = 120 - 10 - 30 = 80 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} p_1 = 90 - 20 - 10 = 60; \\ p_2 = 120 - 10 - 30 = 80 \end{gathered} \]
\[ P_{\max}(1000;\,1000) = 60\,000 + 80\,000 - 20\,000 - 30\,000 - 20\,000 - 8000 = 62\,000 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P_{\max}(1000;\,1000) \\ &= 60\,000 + 80\,000 - 20\,000 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 30\,000 - 20\,000 - 8000 \\ &= 62\,000 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

a) The company should produce 1000 portable and 1000 home speakers a month.

b) The portable speakers should be sold at 60 euros and the home speakers at 80 euros. The maximum profit is 62 000 euros a month.

Example 4

Selling in two markets

Problem A company sells the same coffee machine at home and abroad. If it sells \(x\) machines a month in the home market and \(y\) machines abroad, the prices, in euros, are
\[ p_1 = 152 - 0.04x;\qquad p_2 = 112 - 0.02y \]
\[ \begin{gathered} p_1 = 152 - 0.04x; \\ p_2 = 112 - 0.02y \end{gathered} \]
All the machines are made in one plant. The monthly cost of producing \(Q\) machines is \[ TC = 8000 + 30Q + 0.005Q^2 \] euros. How many machines should be sold in each market, and at what prices, to maximize the profit? What is the maximum profit?

1The objective function

The revenue comes from two markets, so the price in each market is multiplied by the quantity sold there:

\[ R(x;\,y) = (152 - 0.04x)x + (112 - 0.02y)y = 152x - 0.04x^2 + 112y - 0.02y^2 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &R(x;\,y) \\ &= (152 - 0.04x)x + (112 - 0.02y)y \\ &= 152x - 0.04x^2 + 112y - 0.02y^2 \end{aligned} \]

The cost depends on the total number of machines produced, \(Q = x + y\):

\[ \begin{aligned} TC(x;\,y) &= 8000 + 30(x + y) + 0.005(x + y)^2 \\ P(x;\,y) &= 152x - 0.04x^2 + 112y - 0.02y^2 - 8000 - 30(x + y) - 0.005(x + y)^2 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} TC(x;\,y) &= 8000 + 30(x + y) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ 0.005(x + y)^2 \\ P(x;\,y) &= 152x - 0.04x^2 + 112y \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 0.02y^2 - 8000 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 30(x + y) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 0.005(x + y)^2 \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

By the chain rule, \(\big((x + y)^2\big)'_x = 2(x + y)\). The derivative with respect to \(y\) is found in the same way:

\[ \begin{aligned} P'_x &= 152 - 0.08x - 30 - 0.01(x + y) = 122 - 0.09x - 0.01y \\ P'_y &= 112 - 0.04y - 30 - 0.01(x + y) = 82 - 0.01x - 0.05y \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P'_x &= 152 - 0.08x - 30 - 0.01(x + y) \\ &= 122 - 0.09x - 0.01y \\ P'_y &= 112 - 0.04y - 30 - 0.01(x + y) \\ &= 82 - 0.01x - 0.05y \end{aligned} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 122 - 0.09x - 0.01y &= 0 \\ 82 - 0.01x - 0.05y &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

The equations contain decimals. To make the coefficients integers, we multiply each equation by 100. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 122 - 0.09x - 0.01y = 0\;\big|\cdot 100 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 12\,200 - 9x - y = 0 \\ 82 - 0.01x - 0.05y = 0\;\big|\cdot 100 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 8200 - x - 5y = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 122 - 0.09x - 0.01y = 0\;\big|\cdot 100 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 12\,200 - 9x - y = 0 \\[4pt] 82 - 0.01x - 0.05y = 0\;\big|\cdot 100 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 8200 - x - 5y = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 9x + y &= 12\,200 \\ x + 5y &= 8200 \end{aligned} \right. \]

4Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(y\) is 1, so it is most convenient to express \(y\) from it. We move the term \(9x\) to the right-hand side and change its sign:

\[ 9x + y = 12\,200 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = 12\,200 - 9x \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 9x + y = 12\,200 \\ \Longrightarrow\; y = 12\,200 - 9x \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). Since \(y\) is multiplied by 5 in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(x\):

\[ x + 5(12\,200 - 9x) = 8200 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ x + 61\,000 - 45x = 8200 \;\Longrightarrow\; -44x = 8200 - 61\,000 \;\Longrightarrow\; -44x = -52\,800 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x + 61\,000 - 45x = 8200 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -44x = 8200 - 61\,000 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -44x = -52\,800 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(-44\):

\[ x = \frac{-52\,800}{-44} = 1200 \]

We substitute the value of \(x\) into the expression \(y = 12\,200 - 9x\):

\[ y = 12\,200 - 9\cdot 1200 = 12\,200 - 10\,800 = 1400 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 12\,200 - 9\cdot 1200 \\ &= 12\,200 - 10\,800 = 1400 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(1200;\,1400)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(9\cdot 1200 + 1400 = 12\,200\) and \(1200 + 5\cdot 1400 = 8200\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is 1, so we interchange the equations – this 1 becomes the pivot element:

\[ \left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 5 & 8200 \\ 9 & 1 & 12\,200 \end{array}\right) \]

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-9\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(-44\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 5 & 8200 \\ 9 & 1 & 12\,200 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-9)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 5 & 8200 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & -44 & -61\,600 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,(-44)} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 5 & 8200 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 1400 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 1400\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x + 5\cdot 1400 = 8200 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 1200 \]

The same solution \((1200;\,1400)\) is obtained.

5The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = P''_{xx}(K) = -0.09;\quad B = P''_{xy}(K) = -0.01;\quad C = P''_{yy}(K) = -0.05 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-0.09)\cdot (-0.05) - (-0.01)^2 = 0.0045 - 0.0001 = 0.0044 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= P''_{xx}(K) = -0.09 \\ B &= P''_{xy}(K) = -0.01 \\ C &= P''_{yy}(K) = -0.05 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-0.09)\cdot (-0.05) - (-0.01)^2 \\ &= 0.0045 - 0.0001 = 0.0044 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(1200;\,1400)\) is a maximum point.

6The prices and the maximum profit

\[ p_1 = 152 - 48 = 104;\qquad p_2 = 112 - 28 = 84 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} p_1 = 152 - 48 = 104; \\ p_2 = 112 - 28 = 84 \end{gathered} \]

We calculate the revenue and the cost separately. In total \(Q = 1200 + 1400 = 2600\) machines are produced:

\[ \begin{aligned} R &= 104\cdot 1200 + 84\cdot 1400 = 242\,400 \\ TC &= 8000 + 30\cdot 2600 + 0.005\cdot 2600^2 = 119\,800 \\ P_{\max}(1200;\,1400) &= 242\,400 - 119\,800 = 122\,600 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} R &= 104\cdot 1200 + 84\cdot 1400 = 242\,400 \\ TC &= 8000 + 30\cdot 2600 + 0.005\cdot 2600^2 \\ &= 119\,800 \\ P_{\max} &= 242\,400 - 119\,800 = 122\,600 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The company should sell 1200 machines a month in the home market at 104 euros and 1400 machines abroad at 84 euros. The maximum profit is 122 600 euros a month.

Example 5

Two quarries acting together

Problem Two quarries are the only suppliers of crushed stone in a region. The price depends on the total amount supplied: if the first quarry supplies \(x\) tonnes a week and the second \(y\) tonnes, the price is \[ p = 100 - 0.01(x + y) \] euros per tonne. The weekly costs of the quarries, in euros, are
\[ TC_1 = 10x + 0.01x^2;\qquad TC_2 = 5y + 0.02y^2 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} TC_1 = 10x + 0.01x^2; \\ TC_2 = 5y + 0.02y^2 \end{gathered} \]
The quarries agree to act as one firm and to maximize their total profit. How much should each quarry supply? What should the price be, and what is the maximum total profit?

1The objective function

Both quarries sell at the same price, so the total revenue is the price multiplied by the total amount \(x + y\). The total profit is

\[ \begin{aligned} P(x;\,y) &= \big(100 - 0.01(x + y)\big)(x + y) - (10x + 0.01x^2) - (5y + 0.02y^2) \\ &= 100(x + y) - 0.01(x + y)^2 - 10x - 0.01x^2 - 5y - 0.02y^2 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P(x;\,y) \\ &= \big(100 - 0.01(x + y)\big)(x + y) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- (10x + 0.01x^2) - (5y + 0.02y^2) \\ &= 100(x + y) - 0.01(x + y)^2 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 10x - 0.01x^2 - 5y - 0.02y^2 \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

By the chain rule, the derivative of the expression \(0.01(x + y)^2\) with respect to each variable is \(0.02(x + y)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} P'_x &= 100 - 0.02(x + y) - 10 - 0.02x = 90 - 0.04x - 0.02y \\ P'_y &= 100 - 0.02(x + y) - 5 - 0.04y = 95 - 0.02x - 0.06y \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P'_x &= 100 - 0.02(x + y) - 10 - 0.02x \\ &= 90 - 0.04x - 0.02y \\ P'_y &= 100 - 0.02(x + y) - 5 - 0.04y \\ &= 95 - 0.02x - 0.06y \end{aligned} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 90 - 0.04x - 0.02y &= 0 \\ 95 - 0.02x - 0.06y &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

The equations contain decimals. To make the coefficients integers, we multiply each equation by 50. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 90 - 0.04x - 0.02y = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 4500 - 2x - y = 0 \\ 95 - 0.02x - 0.06y = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 4750 - x - 3y = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 90 - 0.04x - 0.02y = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 4500 - 2x - y = 0 \\[4pt] 95 - 0.02x - 0.06y = 0\;\big|\cdot 50 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 4750 - x - 3y = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 2x + y &= 4500 \\ x + 3y &= 4750 \end{aligned} \right. \]

4Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(y\) is 1, so it is most convenient to express \(y\) from it. We move the term \(2x\) to the right-hand side and change its sign:

\[ 2x + y = 4500 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = 4500 - 2x \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 2x + y = 4500 \\ \Longrightarrow\; y = 4500 - 2x \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). Since \(y\) is multiplied by 3 in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(x\):

\[ x + 3(4500 - 2x) = 4750 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ x + 13\,500 - 6x = 4750 \;\Longrightarrow\; -5x = 4750 - 13\,500 \;\Longrightarrow\; -5x = -8750 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x + 13\,500 - 6x = 4750 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -5x = 4750 - 13\,500 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -5x = -8750 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(-5\):

\[ x = \frac{-8750}{-5} = 1750 \]

We substitute the value of \(x\) into the expression \(y = 4500 - 2x\):

\[ y = 4500 - 2\cdot 1750 = 4500 - 3500 = 1000 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 4500 - 2\cdot 1750 \\ &= 4500 - 3500 = 1000 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(1750;\,1000)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(2\cdot 1750 + 1000 = 4500\) and \(1750 + 3\cdot 1000 = 4750\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is 1, so we interchange the equations – this 1 becomes the pivot element:

\[ \left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 3 & 4750 \\ 2 & 1 & 4500 \end{array}\right) \]

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-2\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(-5\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 3 & 4750 \\ 2 & 1 & 4500 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-2)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & 4750 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & -5 & -5000 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,(-5)} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & 3 & 4750 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 1000 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 1000\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x + 3\cdot 1000 = 4750 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 1750 \]

The same solution \((1750;\,1000)\) is obtained.

5The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = P''_{xx}(K) = -0.04;\quad B = P''_{xy}(K) = -0.02;\quad C = P''_{yy}(K) = -0.06 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-0.04)\cdot (-0.06) - (-0.02)^2 = 0.0024 - 0.0004 = 0.002 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= P''_{xx}(K) = -0.04 \\ B &= P''_{xy}(K) = -0.02 \\ C &= P''_{yy}(K) = -0.06 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-0.04)\cdot (-0.06) - (-0.02)^2 \\ &= 0.0024 - 0.0004 = 0.002 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(1750;\,1000)\) is a maximum point.

6The price and the maximum profit

In total \(1750 + 1000 = 2750\) tonnes are supplied:

\[ p = 100 - 0.01\cdot 2750 = 72.5 \]
\[ P_{\max}(1750;\,1000) = 72.5\cdot 2750 - (17\,500 + 30\,625) - (5000 + 20\,000) = 126\,250 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P_{\max}(1750;\,1000) \\ &= 72.5\cdot 2750 - (17\,500 + 30\,625) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- (5000 + 20\,000) = 126\,250 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The first quarry should supply 1750 tonnes and the second 1000 tonnes a week. The price would be 72.5 euros per tonne, and the maximum total profit is 126 250 euros a week.

Example 6

Prices in a juice bar

Problem A juice bar sells fresh juices and smoothies. When a juice costs \(p\) euros and a smoothie costs \(r\) euros, the bar sells
\[ x = 250 - 40p + 20r;\qquad y = 350 + 20p - 60r \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x = 250 - 40p + 20r; \\ y = 350 + 20p - 60r \end{gathered} \]
juices and smoothies a day. Making one juice costs 2 euros, and making one smoothie costs 1.5 euros. At what prices should the juices and the smoothies be sold to maximize the daily profit? How many of each would be sold, and what is the maximum profit?

1The objective function

The bar chooses the prices, so the profit is a function of the variables \(p\) and \(r\). Each juice sold brings \(p - 2\) euros of profit, and each smoothie \(r - 1.5\) euros:

\[ P(p;\,r) = (p - 2)(250 - 40p + 20r) + (r - 1.5)(350 + 20p - 60r) \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P(p;\,r) \\ &= (p - 2)(250 - 40p + 20r) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ (r - 1.5)(350 + 20p - 60r) \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

For the term in which both factors contain the variable of differentiation we use the product rule. In the other term the first factor is a constant:

\[ \begin{aligned} P'_p &= (250 - 40p + 20r) + (p - 2)\cdot(-40) + (r - 1.5)\cdot 20 = 300 - 80p + 40r \\ P'_r &= (p - 2)\cdot 20 + (350 + 20p - 60r) + (r - 1.5)\cdot(-60) = 400 + 40p - 120r \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P'_p &= (250 - 40p + 20r) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ (p - 2)\cdot(-40) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ (r - 1.5)\cdot 20 \\ &= 300 - 80p + 40r \\[4pt] P'_r &= (p - 2)\cdot 20 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ (350 + 20p - 60r) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ (r - 1.5)\cdot(-60) \\ &= 400 + 40p - 120r \end{aligned} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 300 - 80p + 40r &= 0 \\ 400 + 40p - 120r &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

All the coefficients of the equations are divisible by 40, so, to make the numbers smaller, we divide each equation by 40. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 300 - 80p + 40r = 0\;\big|{:}\,40 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 7.5 - 2p + r = 0 \\ 400 + 40p - 120r = 0\;\big|{:}\,40 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 10 + p - 3r = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 300 - 80p + 40r = 0\;\big|{:}\,40 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 7.5 - 2p + r = 0 \\[4pt] 400 + 40p - 120r = 0\;\big|{:}\,40 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 10 + p - 3r = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 2p - r &= 7.5 \\ -p + 3r &= 10 \end{aligned} \right. \]

4Solving the system by substitution

In the second equation the coefficient of \(p\) is \(-1\), so it is most convenient to express \(p\) from it. We move the term \(-p\) to the right-hand side and the number \(10\) to the left-hand side. The terms that are moved change their signs:

\[ -p + 3r = 10 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3r - 10 = p \;\Longrightarrow\; p = 3r - 10 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} -p + 3r = 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 3r - 10 = p \\ \Longrightarrow\; p = 3r - 10 \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the first equation in place of \(p\). Since \(p\) is multiplied by 2 in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(r\):

\[ 2(3r - 10) - r = 7.5 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ 6r - 20 - r = 7.5 \;\Longrightarrow\; 5r = 7.5 + 20 \;\Longrightarrow\; 5r = 27.5 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 6r - 20 - r = 7.5 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 5r = 7.5 + 20 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 5r = 27.5 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(5\):

\[ r = \frac{27.5}{5} = 5.5 \]

We substitute the value of \(r\) into the expression \(p = 3r - 10\):

\[ p = 3\cdot 5.5 - 10 = 16.5 - 10 = 6.5 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} p &= 3\cdot 5.5 - 10 \\ &= 16.5 - 10 = 6.5 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(6.5;\,5.5)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(2\cdot 6.5 - 5.5 = 7.5\) and \(-6.5 + 3\cdot 5.5 = 10\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is \(-1\). We interchange the equations and divide the new first row by \(-1\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} -1 & 3 & 10 \\ 2 & -1 & 7.5 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,(-1)} \\ \phantom{0} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -3 & -10 \\ 2 & -1 & 7.5 \end{array}\right)\)

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-2\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(5\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -3 & -10 \\ 2 & -1 & 7.5 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-2)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -3 & -10 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & 5 & 27.5 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,5} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -3 & -10 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 5.5 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(r = 5.5\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ p - 3\cdot 5.5 = -10 \;\Longrightarrow\; p = 6.5 \]

The same solution \((6.5;\,5.5)\) is obtained.

5The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = P''_{pp}(K) = -80;\quad B = P''_{pr}(K) = 40;\quad C = P''_{rr}(K) = -120 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-80)\cdot (-120) - 40^2 = 9600 - 1600 = 8000 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= P''_{pp}(K) = -80 \\ B &= P''_{pr}(K) = 40 \\ C &= P''_{rr}(K) = -120 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-80)\cdot (-120) - 40^2 \\ &= 9600 - 1600 = 8000 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(6.5;\,5.5)\) is a maximum point.

6The quantities and the maximum profit

We substitute the prices into the demand formulas and then calculate the profit:

\[ x = 250 - 260 + 110 = 100;\qquad y = 350 + 130 - 330 = 150 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x = 250 - 260 + 110 = 100; \\ y = 350 + 130 - 330 = 150 \end{gathered} \]
\[ P_{\max}(6.5;\,5.5) = 4.5\cdot 100 + 4\cdot 150 = 1050 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} P_{\max}(6.5;\,5.5) &= 4.5\cdot 100 + 4\cdot 150 \\ &= 1050 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

A juice should be sold at 6.5 euros and a smoothie at 5.5 euros. The bar would then sell 100 juices and 150 smoothies a day. The maximum profit is 1050 euros a day.

Example 7

Workers and machines in a toy workshop

Problem A workshop with \(x\) workers and \(y\) rented machines makes
\[ Q(x;\,y) = 40x + 30y + 0.5xy - x^2 - 0.5y^2 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} Q(x;\,y) &= 40x + 30y + 0.5xy \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- x^2 - 0.5y^2 \end{aligned} \]
wooden toys a day. A toy is sold for 12 euros. A worker costs 180 euros a day, the rent of a machine is 120 euros a day, and the fixed cost is 2000 euros a day. How many workers and machines are needed to maximize the daily profit? What would the output be, and what is the maximum profit?

1The objective function

The revenue is the product of the price and the production function . From it we subtract the costs of the inputs and the fixed cost:

\[ \begin{aligned} P(x;\,y) &= 12(40x + 30y + 0.5xy - x^2 - 0.5y^2) - 180x - 120y - 2000 \\ &= 300x + 240y + 6xy - 12x^2 - 6y^2 - 2000 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P(x;\,y) \\ &= 12(40x + 30y + 0.5xy - x^2 - 0.5y^2) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 180x - 120y - 2000 \\ &= 300x + 240y + 6xy - 12x^2 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 6y^2 - 2000 \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

\[ P'_x = 300 + 6y - 24x;\qquad P'_y = 240 + 6x - 12y \]
\[ \begin{gathered} P'_x = 300 + 6y - 24x; \\ P'_y = 240 + 6x - 12y \end{gathered} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 300 + 6y - 24x &= 0 \\ 240 + 6x - 12y &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

All the coefficients of the equations are divisible by 6, so, to make the numbers smaller, we divide each equation by 6. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 300 + 6y - 24x = 0\;\big|{:}\,6 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 50 + y - 4x = 0 \\ 240 + 6x - 12y = 0\;\big|{:}\,6 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 40 + x - 2y = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 300 + 6y - 24x = 0\;\big|{:}\,6 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 50 + y - 4x = 0 \\[4pt] 240 + 6x - 12y = 0\;\big|{:}\,6 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 40 + x - 2y = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 4x - y &= 50 \\ x - 2y &= -40 \end{aligned} \right. \]

4Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(y\) is \(-1\), so it is most convenient to express \(y\) from it. We move the term \(-y\) to the right-hand side and the number \(50\) to the left-hand side. The terms that are moved change their signs:

\[ 4x - y = 50 \;\Longrightarrow\; 4x - 50 = y \;\Longrightarrow\; y = 4x - 50 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 4x - y = 50 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 4x - 50 = y \\ \Longrightarrow\; y = 4x - 50 \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). Since \(y\) is multiplied by \(-2\) in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(x\):

\[ x - 2(4x - 50) = -40 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ x - 8x + 100 = -40 \;\Longrightarrow\; -7x = -40 - 100 \;\Longrightarrow\; -7x = -140 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x - 8x + 100 = -40 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -7x = -40 - 100 \\ \Longrightarrow\; -7x = -140 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(-7\):

\[ x = \frac{-140}{-7} = 20 \]

We substitute the value of \(x\) into the expression \(y = 4x - 50\):

\[ y = 4\cdot 20 - 50 = 80 - 50 = 30 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 4\cdot 20 - 50 \\ &= 80 - 50 = 30 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(20;\,30)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(4\cdot 20 - 30 = 50\) and \(20 - 2\cdot 30 = -40\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is 1, so we interchange the equations – this 1 becomes the pivot element:

\[ \left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -2 & -40 \\ 4 & -1 & 50 \end{array}\right) \]

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-4\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(7\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -2 & -40 \\ 4 & -1 & 50 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-4)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -2 & -40 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & 7 & 210 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,7} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -2 & -40 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 30 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 30\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x - 2\cdot 30 = -40 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 20 \]

The same solution \((20;\,30)\) is obtained.

5The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = P''_{xx}(K) = -24;\quad B = P''_{xy}(K) = 6;\quad C = P''_{yy}(K) = -12 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-24)\cdot (-12) - 6^2 = 288 - 36 = 252 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= P''_{xx}(K) = -24 \\ B &= P''_{xy}(K) = 6 \\ C &= P''_{yy}(K) = -12 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-24)\cdot (-12) - 6^2 \\ &= 288 - 36 = 252 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(20;\,30)\) is a maximum point.

6The output and the maximum profit

\[ \begin{gathered} Q(20;\,30) = 800 + 900 + 300 - 400 - 450 = 1150 \\ P_{\max}(20;\,30) = 12\cdot 1150 - 180\cdot 20 - 120\cdot 30 - 2000 = 4600 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &Q(20;\,30) \\ &= 800 + 900 + 300 - 400 - 450 \\ &= 1150 \\[4pt] &P_{\max}(20;\,30) \\ &= 12\cdot 1150 - 180\cdot 20 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 120\cdot 30 - 2000 \\ &= 4600 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The workshop should employ 20 workers and rent 30 machines. It would then make 1150 toys a day. The maximum profit is 4600 euros a day.

Example 8

Labour and equipment in a craft brewery

Problem A craft brewery produces \[ Q(x;\,y) = 20x^{0.25}y^{0.5} \] bottles of beer a week, where \(x\) is the number of hours of labour and \(y\) is the number of hours the brewing equipment is used. A bottle is sold for 6 euros, an hour of labour costs 20 euros, an hour of the equipment costs 10 euros, and the fixed cost is 500 euros a week. How many hours of labour and of the equipment are needed to maximize the weekly profit? What would the output be, and what is the maximum profit?

1The objective function

\[ P(x;\,y) = 6\cdot 20x^{0.25}y^{0.5} - 20x - 10y - 500 = 120x^{0.25}y^{0.5} - 20x - 10y - 500 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &P(x;\,y) \\ &= 6\cdot 20x^{0.25}y^{0.5} - 20x - 10y - 500 \\ &= 120x^{0.25}y^{0.5} - 20x - 10y - 500 \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

By the power rule:

\[ P'_x = 30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5} - 20;\qquad P'_y = 60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5} - 10 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} P'_x = 30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5} - 20; \\ P'_y = 60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5} - 10 \end{gathered} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero and move the costs of the inputs to the right-hand sides:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5} - 20 &= 0 \\ 60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5} - 10 &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \;\Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} 30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5} &= 20 \\ 60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5} &= 10 \end{aligned} \right. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \left\{ \begin{aligned} 30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5} - 20 &= 0 \\ 60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5} - 10 &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \\ \Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} 30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5} &= 20 \\ 60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5} &= 10 \end{aligned} \right. \end{gathered} \]

This system is not linear: the variables are in powers with fractional exponents, and each equation contains both variables. From such an equation one variable cannot simply be expressed through the other. Therefore Gaussian elimination cannot be used for it – it applies only to systems of linear equations, in which each unknown is of the first degree and is only multiplied by a number.

4Dividing the equations

We divide the first equation by the second: the left-hand side by the left-hand side and the right-hand side by the right-hand side. The coefficients simplify, \(\frac{30}{60} = 0.5\), and powers of the same base are divided by subtracting their exponents: \(x^{-0.75 - 0.25} = x^{-1}\) and \(y^{0.5 - (-0.5)} = y\):

\[ \frac{30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5}}{60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5}} = \frac{20}{10} \;\Longrightarrow\; 0.5\cdot x^{-1}y = 2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 0.5\cdot\frac{y}{x} = 2 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \frac{30x^{-0.75}y^{0.5}}{60x^{0.25}y^{-0.5}} = \frac{20}{10} \\ \Longrightarrow\; 0.5\cdot x^{-1}y = 2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 0.5\cdot\frac{y}{x} = 2 \end{gathered} \]

Multiplying both sides by \(2x\), we express \(y\) through \(x\):

\[ y = 4x \]

5Substituting into an equation

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). The power \((4x)^{-0.5}\) is split into factors: \(4^{-0.5} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4}} = 0.5\), so \((4x)^{-0.5} = 0.5x^{-0.5}\):

\[ 60x^{0.25}(4x)^{-0.5} = 10 \;\Longrightarrow\; 60\cdot 0.5\cdot x^{0.25 - 0.5} = 10 \;\Longrightarrow\; 30x^{-0.25} = 10 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 60x^{0.25}(4x)^{-0.5} = 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 60\cdot 0.5\cdot x^{0.25 - 0.5} = 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 30x^{-0.25} = 10 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides by 30. A negative exponent means the reciprocal, so:

\[ x^{-0.25} = \frac{1}{3} \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{0.25} = 3 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 3^4 = 81 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x^{-0.25} = \frac{1}{3} \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{0.25} = 3 \\ \Longrightarrow\; x = 3^4 = 81 \end{gathered} \]

We substitute \(x\) into the expression \(y = 4x\): \(y = 4\cdot 81 = 324\). We obtain the critical point \(K(81;\,324)\).

6The sufficient condition

The production function is a Cobb-Douglas function with \(A = 20 > 0\) and \(\alpha + \beta = 0.25 + 0.5 = 0.75 \le 1\), so it is concave. The profit function is also concave, so \(K(81;\,324)\) is a maximum point.

7The output and the maximum profit

Since \(81^{0.25} = 3\) and \(324^{0.5} = 18\),

\[ \begin{aligned} Q(81;\,324) &= 20\cdot 3\cdot 18 = 1080 \\ P_{\max}(81;\,324) &= 6\cdot 1080 - 20\cdot 81 - 10\cdot 324 - 500 = 1120 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &Q(81;\,324) \\ &= 20\cdot 3\cdot 18 = 1080 \\[4pt] &P_{\max}(81;\,324) \\ &= 6\cdot 1080 - 20\cdot 81 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 10\cdot 324 - 500 = 1120 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The brewery should use 81 hours of labour and 324 hours of the equipment a week. It would then produce 1080 bottles. The maximum profit is 1120 euros a week.

Example 9

Sales representatives in two regions

Problem A company employs \(x\) sales representatives in region A and \(y\) representatives in region B. The gross profit they bring, in thousands of euros a month, is \[ G(x;\,y) = 60\ln(x + 1) + 90\ln(y + 1) \] A representative costs the company 3 thousand euros a month in region A and 5 thousand euros in region B. How many representatives should be employed in each region to maximize the profit after paying them? What is that profit?

1The objective function

\[ N(x;\,y) = 60\ln(x + 1) + 90\ln(y + 1) - 3x - 5y \]
\[ \begin{aligned} N(x;\,y) &= 60\ln(x + 1) + 90\ln(y + 1) \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 3x - 5y \end{aligned} \]

2The first order partial derivatives

By the chain rule, \(\big(\ln(x + 1)\big)'_x = \frac{1}{x + 1}\). The derivative with respect to \(y\) is found in the same way:

\[ N'_x = \frac{60}{x + 1} - 3;\qquad N'_y = \frac{90}{y + 1} - 5 \]

Each derivative is the difference of a fraction and a number. To subtract, we need a common denominator, so we write the number as a fraction with the denominator \(x + 1\): \(3 = \frac{3(x + 1)}{x + 1}\). Then we subtract the numerators and keep the common denominator:

\[ N'_x = \frac{60}{x + 1} - \frac{3(x + 1)}{x + 1} = \frac{60 - 3(x + 1)}{x + 1} = \frac{60 - 3x - 3}{x + 1} = \frac{57 - 3x}{x + 1} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} N'_x &= \frac{60}{x + 1} - \frac{3(x + 1)}{x + 1} \\ &= \frac{60 - 3(x + 1)}{x + 1} = \frac{60 - 3x - 3}{x + 1} \\ &= \frac{57 - 3x}{x + 1} \end{aligned} \]

In the same way, \(5 = \frac{5(y + 1)}{y + 1}\):

\[ N'_y = \frac{90}{y + 1} - \frac{5(y + 1)}{y + 1} = \frac{90 - 5(y + 1)}{y + 1} = \frac{90 - 5y - 5}{y + 1} = \frac{85 - 5y}{y + 1} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} N'_y &= \frac{90}{y + 1} - \frac{5(y + 1)}{y + 1} \\ &= \frac{90 - 5(y + 1)}{y + 1} = \frac{90 - 5y - 5}{y + 1} \\ &= \frac{85 - 5y}{y + 1} \end{aligned} \]

3The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} \frac{57 - 3x}{x + 1} &= 0 \\ \frac{85 - 5y}{y + 1} &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

This system is not linear: the variables are in the denominators of fractions. Therefore Gaussian elimination cannot be used for it – it applies only to systems of linear equations, in which each unknown is of the first degree and is only multiplied by a number.

However, each equation contains only one variable, so the equations can be solved separately. A fraction is equal to zero when its numerator is equal to zero and its denominator is not equal to zero. Therefore each equation is replaced by two conditions:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 57 - 3x &= 0 \\ x + 1 &\ne 0 \\ 85 - 5y &= 0 \\ y + 1 &\ne 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

4Solving the system

The equations with the numerators set equal to zero give \(x\) and \(y\), and the conditions on the denominators give the values that the variables cannot take:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 57 - 3x &= 0 \\ x + 1 &\ne 0 \\ 85 - 5y &= 0 \\ y + 1 &\ne 0 \end{aligned} \right. \;\Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} 3x &= 57 \\ x &\ne -1 \\ 5y &= 85 \\ y &\ne -1 \end{aligned} \right. \;\Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} x &= 19 \\ x &\ne -1 \\ y &= 17 \\ y &\ne -1 \end{aligned} \right. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \left\{ \begin{aligned} 57 - 3x &= 0 \\ x + 1 &\ne 0 \\ 85 - 5y &= 0 \\ y + 1 &\ne 0 \end{aligned} \right. \;\Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} 3x &= 57 \\ x &\ne -1 \\ 5y &= 85 \\ y &\ne -1 \end{aligned} \right. \\ \Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} x &= 19 \\ x &\ne -1 \\ y &= 17 \\ y &\ne -1 \end{aligned} \right. \end{gathered} \]

The conditions on the denominators show that \(x\) and \(y\) cannot be equal to \(-1\). The solutions found, \(x = 19\) and \(y = 17\), are different from this value, so the conditions are satisfied. Besides, \(-1\) is a negative number, and the number of representatives cannot be negative, so this value is of no interest to us.

We obtain the critical point \(K(19;\,17)\).

5The sufficient condition

This time the second order partial derivatives depend on \(x\) and \(y\), so they are not numbers:

\[ N''_{xx} = -\frac{60}{(x + 1)^2};\qquad N''_{xy} = 0;\qquad N''_{yy} = -\frac{90}{(y + 1)^2} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} N''_{xx} = -\frac{60}{(x + 1)^2}; \\ N''_{xy} = 0; \\ N''_{yy} = -\frac{90}{(y + 1)^2} \end{gathered} \]

We obtain the numbers \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) by substituting the coordinates of the critical point \(K(19;\,17)\), \(x = 19\) and \(y = 17\), into the derivatives:

\[ \begin{aligned} A &= N''_{xx}(K) = -\frac{60}{(19 + 1)^2} = -\frac{60}{400} = -0.15 \\ B &= N''_{xy}(K) = 0 \\ C &= N''_{yy}(K) = -\frac{90}{(17 + 1)^2} = -\frac{90}{324} = -\frac{5}{18} \\ D &= AC - B^2 = (-0.15)\cdot\Big(-\frac{5}{18}\Big) - 0^2 = \frac{1}{24} > 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= N''_{xx}(K) = -\frac{60}{(19 + 1)^2} \\ &= -\frac{60}{400} = -0.15 \\ B &= N''_{xy}(K) = 0 \\ C &= N''_{yy}(K) = -\frac{90}{(17 + 1)^2} \\ &= -\frac{90}{324} = -\frac{5}{18} \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-0.15)\cdot\Big(-\frac{5}{18}\Big) - 0^2 \\ &= \frac{1}{24} > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(19;\,17)\) is a maximum point.

6The maximum profit

\[ N_{\max}(19;\,17) = 60\ln 20 + 90\ln 18 - 57 - 85 \approx 297.88 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &N_{\max}(19;\,17) \\ &= 60\ln 20 + 90\ln 18 - 57 - 85 \\ &\approx 297.88 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The company should employ 19 representatives in region A and 17 representatives in region B. The maximum monthly profit after paying the representatives is approximately 297.88 thousand euros.

Example 10

Settings of a paint mixer

Problem The cost, in euros, of producing one batch of paint depends on the temperature of the mixture \(x\) (in degrees Celsius) and the mixing time \(y\) (in minutes):
\[ TC(x;\,y) = 0.05x^2 + 0.4y^2 - 0.1xy - 1.5x - 16y + 500 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} TC(x;\,y) &= 0.05x^2 + 0.4y^2 - 0.1xy \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 1.5x - 16y + 500 \end{aligned} \]
Find the temperature and the mixing time that minimize the cost of one batch. What is the minimum cost?

1The first order partial derivatives

\[ TC'_x = 0.1x - 0.1y - 1.5;\qquad TC'_y = 0.8y - 0.1x - 16 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} TC'_x &= 0.1x - 0.1y - 1.5 \\ TC'_y &= 0.8y - 0.1x - 16 \end{aligned} \]

2The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 0.1x - 0.1y - 1.5 &= 0 \\ 0.8y - 0.1x - 16 &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

The equations contain decimals. To make the coefficients integers, we multiply each equation by 10. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 0.1x - 0.1y - 1.5 = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \;&\Longrightarrow\; x - y - 15 = 0 \\ 0.8y - 0.1x - 16 = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 8y - x - 160 = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 0.1x - 0.1y - 1.5 = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\; x - y - 15 = 0 \\[4pt] 0.8y - 0.1x - 16 = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 8y - x - 160 = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} x - y &= 15 \\ -x + 8y &= 160 \end{aligned} \right. \]

3Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(x\) is 1, so it is most convenient to express \(x\) from it. We move the term \(-y\) to the right-hand side and change its sign:

\[ x - y = 15 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = y + 15 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} x - y = 15 \\ \Longrightarrow\; x = y + 15 \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(x\). There is a minus sign in front of \(x\), so the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(y\):

\[ -(y + 15) + 8y = 160 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ -y - 15 + 8y = 160 \;\Longrightarrow\; 7y = 160 + 15 \;\Longrightarrow\; 7y = 175 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} -y - 15 + 8y = 160 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 7y = 160 + 15 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 7y = 175 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(7\):

\[ y = \frac{175}{7} = 25 \]

We substitute the value of \(y\) into the expression \(x = y + 15\):

\[ x = 25 + 15 = 40 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} x &= 25 + 15 \\ &= 40 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(40;\,25)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(40 - 25 = 15\) and \(-40 + 8\cdot 25 = 160\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first element of the first row is already 1, so it is the pivot element:

\[ \left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -1 & 15 \\ -1 & 8 & 160 \end{array}\right) \]

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(1\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(7\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -1 & 15 \\ -1 & 8 & 160 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{1} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -1 & 15 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & 7 & 175 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,7} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -1 & 15 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 25 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 25\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x - 25 = 15 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 40 \]

The same solution \((40;\,25)\) is obtained.

4The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = TC''_{xx}(K) = 0.1;\quad B = TC''_{xy}(K) = -0.1;\quad C = TC''_{yy}(K) = 0.8 \\ D = AC - B^2 = 0.1\cdot 0.8 - (-0.1)^2 = 0.08 - 0.01 = 0.07 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= TC''_{xx}(K) = 0.1 \\ B &= TC''_{xy}(K) = -0.1 \\ C &= TC''_{yy}(K) = 0.8 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= 0.1\cdot 0.8 - (-0.1)^2 \\ &= 0.08 - 0.01 = 0.07 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A > 0\), so \(K(40;\,25)\) is a minimum point.

5The minimum cost

\[ TC_{\min}(40;\,25) = 80 + 250 - 100 - 60 - 400 + 500 = 270 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &TC_{\min}(40;\,25) \\ &= 80 + 250 - 100 - 60 - 400 + 500 \\ &= 270 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The paint should be mixed at 40 degrees Celsius for 25 minutes. The minimum cost of one batch is 270 euros.

Example 11

Maintenance of two production lines

Problem A factory has two production lines. If \(x\) hours of maintenance a month are done on line A and \(y\) hours on line B, the monthly cost of maintenance and of breakdowns, in euros, is
\[ TC(x;\,y) = 200x + 300y + 40\,000e^{-0.1x} + 90\,000e^{-0.1y} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &TC(x;\,y) \\ &= 200x + 300y \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ 40\,000e^{-0.1x} + 90\,000e^{-0.1y} \end{aligned} \]
How many hours of maintenance on each line minimize the cost? What is the minimum cost?

1The first order partial derivatives

By the chain rule, \(\big(e^{-0.1x}\big)'_x = -0.1e^{-0.1x}\):

\[ TC'_x = 200 - 4000e^{-0.1x};\qquad TC'_y = 300 - 9000e^{-0.1y} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} TC'_x = 200 - 4000e^{-0.1x}; \\ TC'_y = 300 - 9000e^{-0.1y} \end{gathered} \]

2The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 200 - 4000e^{-0.1x} &= 0 \\ 300 - 9000e^{-0.1y} &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

This system is not linear: the variables are in the exponents of exponential functions. Therefore Gaussian elimination cannot be used for it – it applies only to systems of linear equations, in which each unknown is of the first degree and is only multiplied by a number.

Each equation contains only one variable, so we solve the equations separately. First we leave the exponential function alone on one side: we move the term containing it to the right-hand side and divide both sides by its coefficient:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 4000e^{-0.1x} &= 200 \\ 9000e^{-0.1y} &= 300 \end{aligned} \right. \;\Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} e^{-0.1x} &= 0.05 \\ e^{-0.1y} &= \frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \left\{ \begin{aligned} 4000e^{-0.1x} &= 200 \\ 9000e^{-0.1y} &= 300 \end{aligned} \right. \\ \Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} e^{-0.1x} &= 0.05 \\ e^{-0.1y} &= \frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \end{gathered} \]

3Solving the system

The variables are in the exponents. To “bring them down”, we take the natural logarithm of both sides of each equation. The equality remains true, because the logarithms of equal numbers are equal:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} e^{-0.1x} &= 0.05 \\ e^{-0.1y} &= \frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \;\Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} \ln e^{-0.1x} &= \ln 0.05 \\ \ln e^{-0.1y} &= \ln\frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \left\{ \begin{aligned} e^{-0.1x} &= 0.05 \\ e^{-0.1y} &= \frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \\ \Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} \ln e^{-0.1x} &= \ln 0.05 \\ \ln e^{-0.1y} &= \ln\frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \end{gathered} \]

For the logarithm of a power we use the general rule – the exponent is brought in front of the logarithm:

\[ \ln\big(a^n\big) = n\ln a \]

On the left-hand sides the base is \(e\), and the exponents are \(-0.1x\) and \(-0.1y\), so \(\ln e^{-0.1x} = -0.1x\cdot\ln e\) and \(\ln e^{-0.1y} = -0.1y\cdot\ln e\). Besides, \(\ln e = 1\), because the base of the natural logarithm is the number \(e\):

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -0.1x\cdot\ln e &= \ln 0.05 \\ -0.1y\cdot\ln e &= \ln\frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \;\Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} -0.1x &= \ln 0.05 \\ -0.1y &= \ln\frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \left\{ \begin{aligned} -0.1x\cdot\ln e &= \ln 0.05 \\ -0.1y\cdot\ln e &= \ln\frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \\ \Longrightarrow\; \left\{ \begin{aligned} -0.1x &= \ln 0.05 \\ -0.1y &= \ln\frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \end{gathered} \]

We have obtained two simple linear equations. We divide both sides of each equation by \(-0.1\), i.e. multiply them by \(-10\):

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} x &= -10\ln 0.05 \\ y &= -10\ln\frac{1}{30} \end{aligned} \right. \]

We simplify the logarithms with the same rule. Since \(0.05 = \frac{1}{20} = 20^{-1}\), we get \(\ln 0.05 = \ln 20^{-1} = -\ln 20\). In the same way, \(\ln\frac{1}{30} = \ln 30^{-1} = -\ln 30\). Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} x &= -10\cdot(-\ln 20) = 10\ln 20 \approx 29.96 \\ y &= -10\cdot(-\ln 30) = 10\ln 30 \approx 34.01 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(10\ln 20;\,10\ln 30)\), approximately \(K(29.96;\,34.01)\).

4The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives depend on \(x\) and \(y\):

\[ TC''_{xx} = 400e^{-0.1x};\qquad TC''_{xy} = 0;\qquad TC''_{yy} = 900e^{-0.1y} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} TC''_{xx} = 400e^{-0.1x}; \\ TC''_{xy} = 0; \\ TC''_{yy} = 900e^{-0.1y} \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the coordinates of the critical point \(K(10\ln 20;\,10\ln 30)\). From the system of equations we know that at this point \(e^{-0.1x} = 0.05\) and \(e^{-0.1y} = \frac{1}{30}\), so

\[ \begin{aligned} A &= TC''_{xx}(K) = 400\cdot 0.05 = 20 \\ B &= TC''_{xy}(K) = 0 \\ C &= TC''_{yy}(K) = 900\cdot\frac{1}{30} = 30 \\ D &= AC - B^2 = 20\cdot 30 - 0^2 = 600 > 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= TC''_{xx}(K) = 400\cdot 0.05 = 20 \\ B &= TC''_{xy}(K) = 0 \\ C &= TC''_{yy}(K) = 900\cdot\frac{1}{30} = 30 \\ D &= AC - B^2 = 20\cdot 30 - 0^2 \\ &= 600 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A > 0\), so \(K(10\ln 20;\,10\ln 30)\) is a minimum point.

\[ TC_{\min} = 200\cdot 10\ln 20 + 300\cdot 10\ln 30 + 40\,000\cdot 0.05 + 90\,000\cdot\frac{1}{30} \approx 21\,195.06 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} TC_{\min} &= 200\cdot 10\ln 20 + 300\cdot 10\ln 30 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}+ 40\,000\cdot 0.05 + 90\,000\cdot\frac{1}{30} \\ &\approx 21\,195.06 \end{aligned} \]

Example 12

Fertiliser and irrigation

Problem The yield of wheat, in kilograms per hectare, depends on the amount of nitrogen fertiliser \(x\) (in kilograms per hectare) and the amount of irrigation water \(y\) (in millimetres):
\[ Y(x;\,y) = 2000 + 35x + 35y - 0.2x^2 - 0.1y^2 + 0.1xy \]
\[ \begin{aligned} Y(x;\,y) &= 2000 + 35x + 35y \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 0.2x^2 - 0.1y^2 + 0.1xy \end{aligned} \]
Find the amounts of fertiliser and water that maximize the yield. What is the maximum yield?

1The first order partial derivatives

\[ Y'_x = 35 - 0.4x + 0.1y;\qquad Y'_y = 35 - 0.2y + 0.1x \]
\[ \begin{aligned} Y'_x &= 35 - 0.4x + 0.1y \\ Y'_y &= 35 - 0.2y + 0.1x \end{aligned} \]

2The system of equations

We set both partial derivatives equal to zero:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 35 - 0.4x + 0.1y &= 0 \\ 35 - 0.2y + 0.1x &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

The equations contain decimals. To make the coefficients integers, we multiply each equation by 10. Every term of the equation is transformed in this way, including the constant term:

\[ \begin{aligned} 35 - 0.4x + 0.1y = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 350 - 4x + y = 0 \\ 35 - 0.2y + 0.1x = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \;&\Longrightarrow\; 350 - 2y + x = 0 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 35 - 0.4x + 0.1y = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 350 - 4x + y = 0 \\[4pt] 35 - 0.2y + 0.1x = 0\;\big|\cdot 10 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 350 - 2y + x = 0 \end{gathered} \]

We keep the variables on the left-hand side and the numbers on the right-hand side. When a term is moved to the other side of the equation, its sign changes. This gives a system of linear equations:

\[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} 4x - y &= 350 \\ -x + 2y &= 350 \end{aligned} \right. \]

3Solving the system by substitution

In the first equation the coefficient of \(y\) is \(-1\), so it is most convenient to express \(y\) from it. We move the term \(-y\) to the right-hand side and the number \(350\) to the left-hand side. The terms that are moved change their signs:

\[ 4x - y = 350 \;\Longrightarrow\; 4x - 350 = y \;\Longrightarrow\; y = 4x - 350 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} 4x - y = 350 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 4x - 350 = y \\ \Longrightarrow\; y = 4x - 350 \end{gathered} \]

We substitute the expression into the second equation in place of \(y\). Since \(y\) is multiplied by 2 in that equation, the substituted expression is written in brackets. This gives an equation in one variable \(x\):

\[ -x + 2(4x - 350) = 350 \]

We expand the brackets, collect like terms and move the number to the right-hand side, changing its sign:

\[ -x + 8x - 700 = 350 \;\Longrightarrow\; 7x = 350 + 700 \;\Longrightarrow\; 7x = 1050 \]
\[ \begin{gathered} -x + 8x - 700 = 350 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 7x = 350 + 700 \\ \Longrightarrow\; 7x = 1050 \end{gathered} \]

We divide both sides of the equation by \(7\):

\[ x = \frac{1050}{7} = 150 \]

We substitute the value of \(x\) into the expression \(y = 4x - 350\):

\[ y = 4\cdot 150 - 350 = 600 - 350 = 250 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 4\cdot 150 - 350 \\ &= 600 - 350 = 250 \end{aligned} \]

We obtain the critical point \(K(150;\,250)\). We check it by substituting the values into both equations: \(4\cdot 150 - 250 = 350\) and \(-150 + 2\cdot 250 = 350\) – both equalities hold.

This system is linear, so it can also be solved by Gaussian elimination. If you prefer this method, the solution is given below.

Solution by Gaussian elimination

The augmented matrix of the system is made up of the coefficients of the unknowns and the constant terms (see Topic 3).

The first coefficient of the second equation is \(-1\). We interchange the equations and divide the new first row by \(-1\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} -1 & 2 & 350 \\ 4 & -1 & 350 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,(-1)} \\ \phantom{0} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -2 & -350 \\ 4 & -1 & 350 \end{array}\right)\)

To get 0 below the pivot element, we multiply the first row by \(-4\) and add it to the second row, and then divide the new second row by \(7\):

\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} \htmlClass{hd}{1} & -2 & -350 \\ 4 & -1 & 350 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{c} \htmlClass{ha}{(-4)} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{\downarrow} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -2 & -350 \\ \htmlClass{hc}{0} & 7 & 1750 \end{array}\right)\begin{array}{l} \phantom{0} \\ \htmlClass{ha}{{:}\,7} \end{array}\)\(\Longrightarrow\)\(\left(\begin{array}{cc|c} 1 & -2 & -350 \\ 0 & \htmlClass{hd}{1} & 250 \end{array}\right)\)

Back substitution: the second row gives \(y = 250\). We substitute this value into the first row:

\[ x - 2\cdot 250 = -350 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 150 \]

The same solution \((150;\,250)\) is obtained.

4The sufficient condition

The second order partial derivatives are constant, so their values at the critical point \(K\) are the same numbers:

\[ \begin{gathered} A = Y''_{xx}(K) = -0.4;\quad B = Y''_{xy}(K) = 0.1;\quad C = Y''_{yy}(K) = -0.2 \\ D = AC - B^2 = (-0.4)\cdot (-0.2) - 0.1^2 = 0.08 - 0.01 = 0.07 > 0 \end{gathered} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} A &= Y''_{xx}(K) = -0.4 \\ B &= Y''_{xy}(K) = 0.1 \\ C &= Y''_{yy}(K) = -0.2 \\ D &= AC - B^2 \\ &= (-0.4)\cdot (-0.2) - 0.1^2 \\ &= 0.08 - 0.01 = 0.07 > 0 \end{aligned} \]

\(D > 0\) and \(A < 0\), so \(K(150;\,250)\) is a maximum point.

5The maximum yield

\[ Y_{\max}(150;\,250) = 2000 + 5250 + 8750 - 4500 - 6250 + 3750 = 9000 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &Y_{\max}(150;\,250) \\ &= 2000 + 5250 + 8750 \\ &\phantom{=}\; {}- 4500 - 6250 + 3750 = 9000 \end{aligned} \]
Answer

The yield is largest with 150 kilograms of fertiliser and 250 millimetres of water per hectare. The maximum yield is 9000 kilograms per hectare.
